In 1700 the regular motion of the planets was evidence for God; Newton thought God had to adjust the orbits now and then. Laplace removed the adjustments. In 1800 the fit between creatures and their environments was the best evidence for a designer; Darwin removed the designer. Disease was widely read as divine judgment before germ theory, and mental illness as possession before psychiatry. The argument is inductive: every time God has been offered as the explanation for a natural fact, a better explanation has arrived, and nothing has gone the other way.

From that pattern the skeptic draws two conclusions. The remaining puzzles, the origin of the universe, of life and of consciousness, will probably close the same way. And a hypothesis that has only ever lost ground was probably never doing any explaining.

Theists agree that gap-filling is bad theology; the phrase "God of the gaps" was coined by a Christian scientist as a warning. Their claim is that the main arguments for God are not about gaps inside science but about what science rests on: that there are laws, that they are intelligible, that there is anything for them to govern. A finished physics would not touch those questions. Plantinga argues further that science itself has no conflict with theism; the conflict is with the add-on claim that science is all there is.

What an outsider should take from it: a strong reason to distrust any argument for God that depends on something science has not yet explained, and a real but weaker reason to expect the deeper questions to go the same way. The two sides here are really disagreeing about which questions are of which kind.
