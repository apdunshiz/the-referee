Physics describes the world in terms of structure and function: what things do and how they relate. Experience, the redness of red or the ache of a headache, is not obviously a matter of structure or function at all. That is the hard problem. Most philosophers, believers and non-believers alike, recognise it as a real problem, though about a third deny there is a hard problem at all. Nagel, an atheist, argued that it signals something missing from the scientific picture of the world.

The theistic version of the argument says that if consciousness cannot be built out of non-conscious ingredients, then mind is a fundamental feature of reality, and a reality whose ground is a mind explains that better than one whose ground is matter.

The naturalist has two answers. One is to say that consciousness will be explained as other mysteries were, or that the "hard problem" is an illusion created by how we think about our own minds. The other is to accept that mind is fundamental, as panpsychists do, without adding a God.

What an outsider should take from it: this is a real puzzle that embarrasses the simplest forms of materialism. It is weaker as evidence for God because the obvious alternatives, "we will solve it" and "mind is basic but there is no God", are both open and both held by capable people.
