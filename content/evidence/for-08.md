This is the one piece of evidence on the "for" side that points to a particular religion rather than to a creator in general, and it is where this site's deep dives spend most of their time. The facts most historians grant are these: Jesus existed and was crucified under Pilate; within a few years a creed was circulating that listed appearances to Peter, the Twelve, five hundred people and James, to which Paul added his own; Paul had persecuted the movement before his experience; Mark reports that his family thought him out of his mind and John that his brothers did not believe in him, which many historians take to include James and some doubt.

The dispute is about explanation. Christians say the best explanation is that Jesus rose. Non-Christian historians mostly say the disciples had experiences they took to be Jesus, as bereaved people often do, and that the stories grew from there. Each side has to pay a price: the Christian must accept a miracle, which is very improbable on any prior that does not already allow for God; the skeptic must accept that several independent people, including two who had not been followers, had such experiences in a short span and that an empty tomb story arose alongside them.

How you weigh it depends almost entirely on what you already think about God. If God exists, a resurrection is the kind of thing that might happen, and the evidence is good. If God does not, no amount of first-century testimony will carry a miracle, and a vision theory is far more likely.

What an outsider should take from it: the historical core is solid, the conclusion is contested, and this is the point at which the general case for a God turns into a case for one religion. The deep dives below grade each supporting claim separately.
