Naturalism is the view an outsider lands on by default if none of the religions persuades them, so it has to be on the table and scored by the same questions. It is not the absence of a worldview. It makes claims: that the physical is all there is, that minds are what brains do, that morality is a human achievement rather than a cosmic fact.

What it does best is explain the texture of the world. The age and scale of the universe, the slow emergence of life, the dependence of mind on brain, the spread and variety of religions: naturalism predicts all of these and the religions mostly had to absorb them after the fact.

What it does worst is explain the frame. Why is there a universe at all, and why one with these constants? Why does any arrangement of matter feel like something from the inside? Why does "you must not" bind anyone? Naturalists answer with brute facts, future science, or a redefinition of the question. Those are legitimate moves but they are costs, and they are exactly the places where the evidence "for" lists live.

An outsider notices that naturalism scores well because the criteria reward testability and historical footing, where it is strongest, and that its competitors score higher on explanatory reach. Which of those matters more is the choice the reader has to make.
