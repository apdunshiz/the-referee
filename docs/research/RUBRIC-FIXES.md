# Three remaining rubric problems: proposed fixes (draft for Alex, 2026-10-28)

Each fix is a rule-text change in content/site.json, under 600 characters. Effects are estimates from the current `why` lines; whichever fixes Alex picks get a sourced research pass before any score moves, as the moral rule did.

Current totals: Naturalism 30, Buddhism 29, Deism 27, Christianity 26, Islam 26, Judaism 25, Hinduism 24.

## 1. Explanation: "inherited cosmology counts" contradicts "a refusal is not an answer"

Problem. The rule says an honest refusal to answer is not an answer, then says an inherited cosmology counts as an answer for everyone who has one. Buddhism refuses the cosmos question outright (the unanswered questions, MN 63) and carries a cyclic cosmology its own modern teachers disown, yet takes the point; naturalism's "brute fact" takes nothing. The naturalism report adds that any one-phrase answer counts, so the point depends on the scorer's taste.

Fix (recommended): define an answer.
> Four questions: a lawful cosmos, conscious minds, moral obligation, suffering. Start at 2; add 1 for each question the worldview answers. An answer names a cause, a mechanism or a necessity that the tradition's own best statement still asserts; a refusal to answer, however principled, is not one, and an inherited cosmology counts only where the tradition still asserts it. Add 1 more, once, if any of its answers is treated by philosophers across the divide as a distinctive resource.

Effect: Buddhism loses the cosmos point (explanation 7→6, total 28). Hinduism still asserts cyclic creation and keeps it. Naturalism's brute fact stays a refusal. Nobody else moves.

Alternative: leave the rule and reword Buddhism's `why` to say the inherited cosmology is credited by the rule, which is what it says now. No totals move.

## 2. Testability: exposure is set by hand, and one experiment moves the gap by two

Problem. Exposure (0 to 5) has no stated scale: naturalism's 4 and deism's 2 rest on the same refuter, a confirmed miracle. Then every kind held or failed moves a point, so the most checkable tradition (Christianity, exposure 4) ends lowest after its deductions. The prayer trials cost every theism a point and earn naturalism a point, so a single experiment moves the naturalism-theism gap by two.

Fix (recommended): list the refuters, and count the prayer trials once.
> Exposure is the number of kinds of refuter the central claim admits, 0 to 5: a historical finding; a controlled experiment; a public prediction with a date; an observation anyone can make today; a logical demonstration. Then add 1 for each kind of checkable claim that has held and subtract 1 for each kind that has failed. Each kind is scored once: foundation claims already scored under history are not scored again; the prayer trials count once, as a failed kind for every tradition that promises answered prayer, and not as a held kind for anyone; meditation and rebirth-memory research count the same way for every tradition that makes those claims.

Effect (estimates, refuter counts to be set by research): Naturalism exposure 3 and held 1 (no supernatural effect), testability 6→4, total 28. Christianity exposure 5, minus 2, testability 2→3, total 27. Islam exposure 5, plus 1 minus 2, testability 2→4, total 28. Judaism exposure 4, minus 1, 2→3, total 26. Hinduism exposure 3, plus 1 minus 1, 1→3, total 26. Buddhism exposure 3, unchanged at 3. Deism exposure 3, nothing held (Alex's earlier ruling stands), 2→3, total 28.

Alternative: keep hand-set exposure and only count the prayer trials once. Naturalism 6→5, total 29; nobody else moves.

## 3. History: "early" is undefined

Problem. Islam's text is early relative to its founder, Hinduism's Vedas are early in absolute terms, Judaism's Torah is early in neither sense (final form in the Persian period, centuries after the events it narrates) yet all three take the point; Buddhism's canon, fixed in writing four centuries after the founder, does not. The Buddhism report notes orality is then charged twice (no early text, plus the late-biography deduction).

Fix (recommended): early means close to the founder.
> An early, stable text is one fixed within two generations of the founder or founding events and transmitted without substantive change since; for a tradition with no founder, the oldest stratum of its canon counts if it has been transmitted stably.

Effect: Judaism loses the point (history 5→4, total 24). Christianity (creed and Paul within 25 years), Islam (within a generation) and Hinduism (no founder; the Rigveda) keep it. Buddhism stays uncredited.

Alternative: early means stable since first fixing, whenever that was, with the gap to the founder scored only under late biography. Buddhism gains the point (history 6→7, total 30); Judaism keeps it. Orality is then charged once.
