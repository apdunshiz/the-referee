# Deism: re-examination of the five scores

## Summary

Current total 22. Proposed total 29 (history 5→6, consistency 7, explanation 3→5, testability 2→4, moral 5→7). Conservative floor if the owner rejects the two contestable moves (crediting suffering under explanation; counting the Cult of the Supreme Being as a state built on deism): 25.

Everything turns on one choice the file makes silently. The site scores every other worldview as a historical tradition, by its best expression and its record, but it scores deism as a bare proposition ("a creator who does not intervene") with no texts, communities or record. That is not what deism was. From Herbert of Cherbury (1624) through Tindal (1730), Voltaire, Paine (1794) and the French civic cults (1794, 1796-1801), deism was "natural religion": a creator known by reason, a moral law known by reason, and for most of its classic defenders rewards and punishments after death [1][2][9]. Scored the way the site scores naturalism, which also has no founder and no scripture, deism picks up the same credits naturalism gets for the scientific record and for the no-miracle and null-prayer results, and it gets a record to be debited under the moral rule. The research confirms the consistency score and the "no distinctive resource" judgment, and it confirms that deism's testable core is weaker than naturalism's on the creator half. What changes is parity: the same kind of fact must earn the same point.

## Historical footing

**Current:** 5, confidence 60. *"Start at 5. It rests on no events, texts, community or scientific record of its own; the cosmological arguments it draws on are arguments, credited under explanation. Nothing holds and nothing has failed. Five."*

**Evidence:**
- Deism rests on the scientific record of a lawful cosmos in the same way naturalism rests on cosmic history. Its 18th-century form was explicitly Newtonian: a universe of uniform law that a perfect workman would not need to adjust, the point Leibniz pressed against Clarke in 1715-16 [10]. Its modern form (Flew) rests on "the origin of the laws of nature, the organization of life, and the origin of life" [5].
- The cosmic half of that record has held. Steady-state cosmology (Hoyle, Bondi, Gold, 1948), which fitted an uncreated universe, was abandoned after the 1965 microwave background; the big-bang model is standard [11]. On constants, "physicists who have written on fine-tuning agree with Smolin that it cries out for an explanation" [4]; the SEP treats the design inference from fine-tuning as live and contested on both sides (Swinburne, Collins, White against Sober, Stenger, Weisberg) [3].
- The biological half went against it. Paine argued that "neither could any tree, plant, or animal make itself" [7]; Darwin's natural selection "unaided by intention or intervention could account for the existence of many" such features, and "what had earlier appeared to be purpose... was now apparently revealed as mere unintended but successful and preserved function" [4]. Griffiths: of the design argument as Paine and Voltaire used it, "Darwin's theory is probably fatal" [8].
- No early stable text: Tindal's book is called "the deist's Bible" because almost every later argument appears in it, but no deist treated it as scripture [2]. No continuous community: Hudson describes English deism as a "failed ideology which itself declined after 1730" [12]; Griffiths says it "founded no churches" [8].
- Named founder-figures exist and are well attested (Herbert, Toland, Tindal, Paine), but deism claims no founding event, so the "well-attested founder or founding event" line neither counts nor costs.

**Rule applied:** Start at 5. Holds: the scientific record of a lawful, non-eternal cosmos, which the worldview rests on (+1). Gone against: the biological design claim is an argument, not a foundational historical claim, so it is not debited here; it is debited once under testability (see below). Six.

**Proposed:** 5→6. Strongest reason: naturalism's file gives +1 because "its one foundation is the scientific record... which holds under scrutiny"; deism's one foundation is the same record, and the rule says "the scientific record where the worldview rests on it." Confidence 55.

**If the owner disagrees:** He would have to hold that deism rests on an inference from the record rather than on the record, while naturalism rests on the record itself. That is a defensible line, but naturalism's "minds are what brains do" is also an inference from the record.

## Internal consistency

**Current:** 7, confidence 60. *"Start at 8. One tension needs careful definition: why a mind that cared enough to create would not care enough to communicate, and if it does not care, why call it God. Seven."*

**Evidence:**
- The tradition's own best statement of non-intervention is Leibniz's: a God who had to mend or rewind his work would be "an imperfect workman"; miracles meet the needs of grace, not of nature [10]. Deists took the next step and dropped grace. Tindal: God "requires nothing for his own sake," and the religion of nature is perfect, so revelation "can add nothing" [2]. This gives a coherent reason for silence, but it is a careful definition, which is exactly what the rule deducts for.
- Fine-tuning plus non-intervention is the same tension restated, not a second one: if the constants were set for life, the creator intended minds, and the question is why it then says nothing to them. One deduction covers both.
- The moral law is a possible second doctrine. Herbert's fifth common notion is that "reward and punishment follow from the goodness and justice of God, both in this life and after it" [1]; Paine: "I hope for happiness beyond this life" [7]; the 1794 decree pairs the Supreme Being with "the immortality of the soul" [6]. A creator who judges after death acts, which the site's one-line definition ("does not intervene") excludes. Flew's version drops the afterlife [5] and then has naturalism's problem: what makes the law bind.
- The brief's question, "is a creator who never acts coherent with a creator who made moral law," has a clean deist answer: the law is built into our nature and read by reason, with natural consequences as its sanction (Tindal). That is one more careful definition, but it is the same one already counted: a creator who provides for virtue without ever speaking.

**Rule applied:** Start at 8. One core doctrine needs careful definition: a creator interested enough to design for minds and virtue but not to communicate or act (−1). The moral-law and fine-tuning tensions are the same doctrine from two sides and are not counted twice. Seven.

**Proposed:** keep 7. Strongest reason: every tension the brief lists collapses into the one the file already deducts; a second deduction would be a double count. Confidence 55.

**If the owner disagrees:** Whether "a law from a lawgiver who never judges" is a separate doctrine from "a creator who never speaks." If he counts it separately (as naturalism is deducted for "binding obligation in a world of particles"), six.

## Explanatory reach

**Current:** 3, confidence 60. *"Start at 2. Answers one question, a lawful cosmos, by taking the origin and fine-tuning evidence at face value (plus 1). Says nothing about minds, obligation or suffering, since the creator is absent after the first moment. No answer is treated across the divide as distinctive. Three."*

**Evidence:**
- Lawful cosmos: answered, as the file says, and the answer is the one "many regard... as the strongest version of the teleological argument that contemporary science affords" [3].
- Moral obligation: answered by every classic deist. Herbert: "virtue combined with piety is the chief part of divine worship" [1]. Tindal: the law of nature is complete and obligatory, "the gospel added nothing to the law of nature" [2]. Paine: "religious duties consist in doing justice, loving mercy, and endeavoring to make our fellow-creatures happy" [7]. The 1794 decree: "the worship worthy of the Supreme Being is the practice of the duties of man" [6]. The file's own moral section calls deism's best expression "an ethic of reason and tolerance," which concedes it has one. Griffiths notes that modern deist ethics is "largely indistinguishable from the humanism... of atheists like A.C. Grayling and Sam Harris" [8]; naturalism was credited for that same answer.
- Suffering: the deist answer is that a lawful world runs without exception and the creator does not suspend it. That is the mechanism naturalism was credited for ("an indifferent nature explains it completely"). The 18th-century deist theodicy was Pope's "whatever is, is right"; Voltaire's Lisbon poem (1756) rejects that ("'T is mockery to tell me all is well") while keeping the creator ("God I respect, yet love the universe... I do not fling myself 'gainst Providence") [13]. So the tradition has both an answer and its own best critic. This is the weaker of the two additions: the question is sharper for deism than for naturalism, since its creator is not indifferent, and Voltaire's position is close to an honest refusal.
- Conscious minds: not answered. Herbert and Paine assumed a soul; Jefferson was a materialist; Flew rejects an afterlife [5]. There is no deist account of why matter is conscious, and the site's core claim is silent.
- Distinctive resource: none. The fine-tuning answer is theism's generally; philosophers across the divide treat it as a resource for theism, not for deism in particular.

**Rule applied:** Start at 2. Answers a lawful cosmos (+1), moral obligation through a creator whose law reason reads (+1), suffering through a lawful world the creator does not suspend (+1). Minds: no answer. No distinctive-resource bonus. Five.

**Proposed:** 3→5. Strongest reason: the file says deism "says nothing about... obligation," and every classic deist text says the opposite; natural religion was defined by its moral law. Confidence 50 (65 for obligation alone; the suffering point is a parity call).

**If the owner disagrees:** Whether "the creator does not intervene, so suffering is what lawful nature produces" is an answer or an honest refusal. If a refusal, four. Note that the same reading would have to be applied to naturalism's "indifferent nature."

## Testable claims

**Current:** 2, confidence 70. *"Exposure 2: a creator could be disconfirmed only by showing the universe necessary. It makes no checkable claims after the creation, so nothing has held or failed. Two."*

**Evidence:**
- The central claim has two halves. The creator half is barely exposed, as the file says. The non-intervention half is as exposed as naturalism's: one confirmed miracle or one demonstrated answer to prayer refutes it. Naturalism's file sets exposure 4 on exactly that ground. Paine stakes the position on it: the supernatural parts of scripture have "every mark of fraud and imposition," and petitionary prayer is absurd ("when it is sunshine, he prays for rain, and when it is rain, he prays for sunshine") [7].
- Held: no miracle has been confirmed to skeptical investigators. Naturalism gets +1 for this; deism predicts the same result.
- Held: the STEP trial (Benson et al., 2006), 1,802 bypass patients at six hospitals, found "intercessory prayer itself had no effect on complication-free recovery" (complications 52% prayed-for vs 51% not, RR 1.02, 95% CI 0.92-1.15) [14]. Naturalism's file counts "the prayer trials' null result" as held; deism predicted it in 1794.
- Held, arguably: a universe with a beginning. Steady-state cosmology would have suited an uncreated universe and failed in 1965 [11]. This is folded into the exposure number rather than counted separately, since a beginning does not entail a creator and the cosmic record is already credited under history.
- Failed: design in living things. Paine's "neither could any tree, plant, or animal make itself" [7] was a checkable claim about biology, and natural selection answered it [4]. This is not a foundation claim scored under history, so it is scored once here.
- Mind-tracks-brain is neutral: the site's deism claims no soul.

**Rule applied:** Exposure 3: the non-intervention half can be refuted by a confirmed miracle or a positive prayer trial; the creator half only by showing the universe necessary. Held, +1 each: no miracle confirmed; prayer trials null. Failed, −1: design in organisms, answered by natural selection. Four.

**Proposed:** 2→4. Strongest reason: naturalism is credited +1 for "no miracle confirmed" and +1 for "the prayer trials' null result"; deism makes the identical predictions and the rule says each kind of claim is scored the same way for every tradition that makes it. Confidence 45.

**If the owner disagrees:** Whether "no miracles" and "no answered prayer" are two kinds of claim or one (non-intervention). If one, three; and naturalism's seven should drop to six for the same reason.

## Moral fruit

**Current:** 5, confidence 55. *"Its best expression is an ethic of reason and tolerance, but it has no communities and no record to credit or debit, so the rule's no-record value applies. Five."*

**Evidence:**
- Best expression extends to outsiders: Paine's duties are "to make our fellow-creatures happy" [7]; Voltaire held that a rational person treats everyone equally [8]; Herbert's common notions were offered as the religion of all men [1].
- Deism has communities and had a state. On 18 Floréal Year II (7 May 1794) the Convention decreed "The French people recognize the existence of the Supreme Being and the immortality of the soul," with worship defined as "the practice of the duties of man"; Robespierre presided as its high priest at the Festival of 8 June [6]. Historians describe the cult as "centered around deism" while debating whether it was a political tool or a true deist religion [6].
- The state built on it persecuted. The Law of 22 Prairial, introduced two days after the Festival by Robespierre's allies, removed the investigation phase and allowed trial on denunciation alone; "over 1,400 people were rapidly guillotined in Paris" in the Great Terror that followed, until Thermidor (27 July 1794) [6][15]. Robespierre had also executed nineteen leading Hébertists in March 1794, partly over the atheist Cult of Reason [6].
- A second deist community, Theophilanthropy (1796), was backed by the Directory through La Révellière-Lépeaux from April 1797, lost support in 1798 and was banned under Napoleon in 1801-02 [16]. It has no record of coercion. Organised deism today is small (World Union of Deists, 1993; Church of the Modern Deist) [8][17].
- Wars and conquest in deism's name: none. The revolutionary wars were declared in 1792, before the cult, and not in its name.
- Exclusion written into its structure: none; deism has no texts to sanction one. Jefferson's slave-holding is a personal fact, not a doctrine, and the attribution to Paine of the 1775 anti-slavery essay is itself disputed [18].
- Parity: naturalism is debited twice for "states built on it, the Soviet and Maoist regimes," though neither state professed naturalism by name. The Jacobin republic professed deism by decree.

**Rule applied:** Start at 8: the ethic extends to outsiders. Wars and conquest in its name: none. Persecution of dissenters by a state built on it: the Great Terror under the Cult of the Supreme Being (−1). Exclusion written into its structure: none. Seven.

**Proposed:** 5→7. Strongest reason: the file's premise, "no communities and no record," is false; a state proclaimed deism as its civic religion and ran the Great Terror under it, and the rule debits "states built on it" exactly as it does for naturalism. Confidence 45.

**If the owner disagrees:** Whether a civic cult of six weeks, which the Terror preceded, counts as a state "built on" deism. If not, the rule returns 5, and the owner should note that a worldview with no record is then scored below two worldviews (naturalism 6, Judaism 7) that have persecutions on theirs.

## Calibration check

1. **Scientific record as foundation.** Naturalism: *"its one foundation is the scientific record of cosmic history, evolution and the brain, which holds under scrutiny (plus 1)."* Deism: *"It rests on no events, texts, community or scientific record of its own; the cosmological arguments it draws on are arguments."* Both rest on cosmic history; naturalism's "minds are what brains do" is as much an inference from the record as deism's "order implies a mind." Reconcile toward deism 6.

2. **No-miracle and null-prayer results.** Naturalism: *"Held claims, plus 1 each: no miracle confirmed to skeptical investigators; mind tracking brain in every detail; the prayer trials' null result."* Deism: *"It makes no checkable claims after the creation, so nothing has held or failed."* Deism's "no intervention" is a checkable claim about every moment after creation, and the two results confirm it. Reconcile toward deism 4, or, if the two are one kind, deism 3 and naturalism 6.

3. **States built on a worldview.** Naturalism: *"Wars and conquest by states built on it, the Soviet and Maoist regimes (minus 1); persecution of dissenters and believers by those states (minus 1)."* Deism: *"it has no communities and no record to credit or debit."* The Jacobin state decreed deism as its religion; the Soviet state decreed Marxism-Leninism, which includes but is not naturalism. If the Soviet case counts, the Jacobin case counts. Reconcile toward deism 7, or drop naturalism's second deduction.

4. **Suffering as "indifferent nature."** Naturalism: *"Answers suffering (an indifferent nature explains it completely)."* Deism: *"Says nothing about... suffering, since the creator is absent after the first moment."* The second sentence is the first answer with a creator in front of it. Reconcile toward deism 4 at least; 5 if obligation is also credited.

## Rule problems (not scored)

- **No record scores lower than a bad record.** The moral rule gives "a stance with no communities and no record" 5, but a tradition with one class of persecution on its record gets 7 (Judaism). A worldview is punished three points for having nobody to persecute. Example: deism at 5 against Judaism at 7.
- **"States built on it" has no test.** The rule debits Soviet and Maoist states to naturalism, the Terror would be debited to deism, and the Crusades to Christianity, but nothing says how much of a state's creed a worldview must be. A written test (the state professed it by decree or constitution, or acted in its name) would make the deductions comparable.
- **The explanation rule rewards saying something over saying it well.** Deism's "the creator does not intervene" and naturalism's "nature is indifferent" earn the same point as Christianity's "God undergoes it" and Hinduism's karma. The rule is explicit that quality is not scored, but the site's question asks "how well."
- **Exposure is set by hand.** Naturalism's 4 and deism's 2 are set on the same refuter (a confirmed miracle) and differ by two points without a stated reason. A short list of what counts as a refuter, with a point each, would remove the hand-setting.
- **The testability rule can double-count or zero-count Darwin.** The biological design argument is deism's historically, but the rule sends it either to history (claim conflicting with the record) or to testability (failed kind) with no guidance; this report chose testability.
