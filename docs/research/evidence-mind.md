The research confirms for-05 and moves the other two lines up by one each, so the headline does not change. J. L. Mackie, the classic atheist critic of theistic arguments, conceded in print that objective values, if they exist, "make the existence of a god more probable than it would have been without them" (Mackie 1982, pp. 115-116). That passes clause (b) for the moral argument, and the SEP's 2022 entry passes (c), so the draft rule puts for-04 at 6, not 5. against-07 also comes out at 6. Its datum is solid, an SEP author presses it, and no survey shows that many specialists hold the one rival that rescues the soul, substance dualism. Its only minus is the contested step from "no soul" to "no God". for-05 stays at 5. Paul Draper's SEP entry explains why these two should sit close together. Draper concedes that the existence of consciousness favours theism, then argues that its detailed dependence on the brain favours physicalism, and concludes that the full evidence "is far from clear" in either direction (Draper 2022, §6.2). These are two separate data pulling opposite ways, so neither should be merged into the other. Some text needs fixing. for-05 overstates agreement on the hard problem and borrows against-07's datum as its objection. against-07 leaves out Gage's documented recovery and overstates what brain injury does. With both proposed changes, each column rises by 1 and the headline stays 43/86 = 50%.

Scoring choice, applied to all three lines: each line is scored as the argument its own file states (summary and body), using the strongest published form of that argument. Where a stronger version with different premises exists in the literature, I say so and give its score too. "Reference work treats it as live" is read the same way for all three: an SEP entry has to present the argument as giving real evidence, even if outweighed, and not as refuted. The Blackwell Companion and Oxford Studies volumes are reported but do not count toward (c), because the rule lists SEP and Oxford or Cambridge handbooks.

Survey figures: "Target" means the full 2020 PhilPapers target population of faculty, with n shown. "Specialist" rows are respondents with that area of specialisation in the 2009-comparable departments, as tabulated by Rob Bensinger from the survey site [3]. Respondents could endorse more than one option, so rows need not sum to 100.

## for-04: Some things seem wrong regardless of what anyone thinks

**Current:** strength 5, confidence 75. Status: "Most philosophers today accept some form of moral realism, but most of them are not theists, so the link from objective morals to God is the contested step."

**Evidence:**
- Moral realism is the majority view. Target: 62% realism, 26% anti-realism (n=1719). Meta-ethicists: 68% vs 22% (n=94). Normative ethicists: 74% vs 16% (n=132) [1][2][3].
- The realism the theistic argument needs most, irreducible, non-natural, intrinsically prescriptive facts, is a minority view. Target: non-naturalism 27%, naturalist realism 32%, constructivism 21%, expressivism 11%, error theory 5% (n=1024). Meta-ethicists: non-naturalism 37% (n=68) [3]. Naturalist realists hold that moral facts are natural facts, which need no lawgiver.
- The status sentence checks out on the survey's own numbers. Theism (accept or lean) is 19% of the target population [1]. Even if every theist were a realist, theists would make up at most about 31% of realists (19/62). So most realists are not theists. This is a check on the text, not evidence for or against God.
- Mackie, the leading 20th-century atheist critic of theistic arguments, granted that objective prescriptive values "constitute so odd a cluster of qualities and relations that they are most unlikely to have arisen in the ordinary course of events, without an all-powerful god to create them. If, then, there are such intrinsically prescriptive objective values, they make the existence of a god more probable than it would have been without them. Thus we have, after all, a defensible inductive argument from morality to the existence of a god" [4, pp. 115-116]. The concession is conditional: Mackie himself denied that objective values exist [5].
- The SEP entry "Moral Arguments for the Existence of God" (Evans and Baggett, revised 2022) treats the argument as live. It says no version is a "proof", but presents the arguments as carrying weight and sets out the standard objections, Euthyphro and godless realism, with theist replies [6].
- Too high: non-theistic robust realism is a developed, university-press position. Wielenberg's *Robust Ethics* (OUP 2014) defends "godless normative realism" [7]. Enoch's *Taking Morality Seriously* (OUP 2011) defends non-naturalism without God [8]. Both give a rival account of the datum.
- Too low: the same SEP entry reports that Enoch and Wielenberg acknowledge Street's evolutionary "Darwinian Dilemma" as "a significant problem for their view" [6][9]. Theists argue that a good creator explains both moral facts and our reliable access to them, which godless realism struggles with (Adams, *Finite and Infinite Goods*, OUP 1999 [10]). Draper, writing for the atheist side, grants that "the existence of moral agents is 'predicted' by omni-theism better than by source physicalism" [11, §6.2].

**Rule applied:** Start at 5.
- (a) holds: realism is the majority view overall (62%) and among meta-ethicists (68%) (+1).
- (b) holds: Mackie, named, grants that objective values would be evidence for a god (+1).
- (c) holds: the SEP entry treats the argument as live (+1).
- (d) holds: the argument needs the premise that objective moral facts require a divine ground. That is a minority view, because most realists are not theists and naturalist realism is the largest single metaethical position (−1).
- (e) holds: non-theistic realism (Wielenberg, Enoch, naturalist realists) is accepted as adequate by many meta-ethicists (−1).
- (f) does not hold: the file's argument concludes to "a moral lawgiver", and Adams' and Evans' versions to a good personal being whose nature is the standard. That conclusion is already personal and good, so the rule's example step, from an impersonal cause to a personal God, is not needed. Borderline: if the owner treats "lawgiver to the God of the religions" as a further contested step, as he may for a "designer" in fine-tuning, this becomes −1 and the line stays at 5.

Total: 5 +1 +1 +1 −1 −1 = **6**.

**Proposed:** change 5 to 6. Strongest reason: Mackie's on-record concession and a live SEP entry together give the argument two plusses that the current 5 does not reflect. Confidence 55, because the result depends on how (f) is read.

**Text fixes:** Nothing in the file is false. If the strength becomes 6, one optional sentence in the body would explain the rise. Add after "...fit naturally into a world made by a good mind.":
> "Even J. L. Mackie, an atheist who denied that objective values exist, conceded that if they did exist they would make God more probable."
(152 characters; the body has no length limit.)

**If the owner disagrees:** he would have to count "a moral lawgiver" as still one contested step short of God, the same (f) he applies to "a designer" in for-02. That one call decides 5 or 6.

## for-05: Conscious experience is hard to fit into a purely physical world

**Current:** strength 5, confidence 65. Status: "The \"hard problem\" of consciousness is widely acknowledged as unsolved, but most philosophers of mind expect a physical or at least non-theistic answer."

**Evidence:**
- Most philosophers say there is a hard problem, and philosophers of mind say so at the same rate. Target: yes 62%, no 30% (n=1468). Philosophy-of-mind specialists: 62% vs 34% (n=91) [1][3]. Chalmers' 1995 paper set the terms [12].
- Most philosophers of mind are physicalists. Specialists: physicalism 60%, non-physicalism 26% (n=135). Target: 52% vs 32% (n=1733). On consciousness, specialists split: functionalism 38%, dualism 24%, identity theory 12%, panpsychism 10%, eliminativism 2% (n=95) [3].
- Neuroscientists are split too. In a 2026 PNAS survey of 2,657 neuroscientists, only 38% agreed that neuroscience will explain subjective experience. The response rate was 1.39% and items were not mutually exclusive, so treat this as indicative only [13].
- Draper, writing the SEP's entry on atheism, grants the datum: "Its existence really does seem to be more likely given omni-theism than given source physicalism." He then argues that the detailed brain-dependence of consciousness cuts the other way, so that the full evidence "is far from clear" to favour theism [11, §6.2].
- Mackie conceded that "having to postulate a fundamental natural law of emergence for awareness is something of a difficulty for the materialist", but argued that theism has "at least as great a difficulty" [4, pp. 130-131].
- Too low: recent peer-reviewed versions do not need dualism. Page argues that consciousness is more probable on theism whether materialism, dualism or panpsychism is true (*Faith and Philosophy* 2020) [14]. Cutter and Crummett's "psychophysical harmony" argument, in OUP's *Oxford Studies in Philosophy of Religion* (2025), claims that the fit between experience and physical states is "strong evidence for theism" and is immune to multiverse replies [15]. Nagel, an atheist, argues that consciousness shows the materialist picture is incomplete [16].
- Too high: illusionists (Frankish 2016 [17]; Dennett [18]) deny that there is a hard problem at all, and panpsychists take mind to be basic without a God. Moreland's defence of the argument appears in the Blackwell Companion [19], which is edited by theists and is not on the rule's list.

**Rule applied (the file's version: "if mind is basic to reality... a universe founded on a mind is less surprising"):** Start at 5.
- (a) holds: that there is a hard problem is the majority view overall (62%) and among philosophers of mind (62%) (+1).
- (b) holds: Draper, named, grants that the existence of consciousness raises the probability of theism over physicalism. Mackie grants that it is a difficulty for materialism (+1).
- (c) holds, on the bar above: Draper's SEP entry treats the argument as giving real evidence, outweighed rather than refuted. The SEP "Philosophy of Religion" entry lists "the emergence of consciousness" among the evidence cited for religious belief [20]. There is no dedicated SEP entry or Oxford or Cambridge handbook chapter, so this (c) is weaker than for-04's (+1).
- (d) holds: the file's version needs mind to be fundamental or irreducible. Only 26% of philosophers of mind are non-physicalists (−1).
- (e) holds: physicalism (60% of specialists) and illusionism (34% deny a hard problem) are accepted by many specialists as adequate in principle. Brain-dependence is not used here, because that is against-07's datum (−1).
- (f) holds: getting from "mind is basic" to a personal God is a further contested step. Panpsychism, cosmopsychism and Nagel's natural teleology take that step without God (−1).

Total: 5 +1 +1 +1 −1 −1 −1 = **5**.

The best published version (Page; Cutter and Crummett) argues straight to theism and does not need non-physicalism. On that version, (d) and (f) arguably do not hold, which gives 6 or 7. Draper's offset is the reason not to score it that high, and the same "best version" choice would also move against-07 (see below).

**Proposed:** keep 5. Strongest reason: both named sides grant the datum, but most specialists think a physicalist or panpsychist account is adequate without a God. Confidence 65.

**Text fixes:**
- Body, "it is recognised as a problem by philosophers on every side" overstates the agreement: 30% say there is no hard problem. Replace with:
  > "Most philosophers, believers and non-believers alike, recognise it as a real problem, though about a third deny there is a hard problem at all."
- best_objection borrows against-07's datum ("consciousness tracks brain states in exact detail"), and "every past gap of this kind closed" overstates. Replace with (321 chars):
  > "Not knowing how brains produce experience is a gap in current science, not evidence for God. Many philosophers of mind think the hard problem is a confusion or an illusion, and panpsychists who take it seriously make mind basic without adding a God. Its close dependence on the brain is a separate datum (see against-07)."
- Body, "the exact dependence of experience on the brain, visible in every anaesthetic and every stroke, shows where the explanation lies" also uses against-07's datum. Replace with:
  > "...is to say that consciousness will be explained as other mysteries were, or that the 'hard problem' is an illusion created by how we think about our own minds."
- Optional addition to the body: "Paul Draper, an agnostic, grants that consciousness is more expected if God exists, but argues that its close dependence on the brain cancels most of that."

**If the owner disagrees:** he would have to count the peer-reviewed likelihood versions (Page, Cutter and Crummett), which need no dualism, as "the best version". That lifts the line to 6 or 7, and the same reading lifts against-07 from 6 to 7.

## against-07: Minds depend on brains in every detail

**Current:** strength 5, confidence 75. Status: "The dependence of mental life on the brain is as well established as anything in neuroscience; theists either accept it and locate survival in resurrection rather than a soul, or argue that dependence shows correlation, not identity."

**Evidence:**
- Draper's SEP entry presents the argument as live evidence against omni-theism. Conscious states "and even the very integrity of our personalities ... are dependent to a very high degree on physical events occurring in the brain ... Given theism, it would not be surprising at all if our minds were more independent of the brain than they in fact are. After all, if omni-theism is true, then at least one mind, God's, does not depend at all on anything physical" [11, §6.2].
- Augustine and Fishman (2015) collect the dependence data from psychopharmacology, developmental psychology and cognitive neuroscience. They argue that dualists must either discount it or accept that survival looks very unlikely [21].
- A clinical example: a right orbitofrontal tumour produced acquired paedophilia that went away when the tumour was removed (Burns and Swerdlow, *Archives of Neurology* 2003) [22]. Mackie used the same fact against the argument from consciousness: theism has to explain "why consciousness is found only in" brains [4, p. 131].
- Too high: the Gage story is weaker than the body tells it. Macmillan and Lena (2010) show that Gage later worked for years as a stagecoach driver in Chile, which suggests substantial social recovery. Macmillan holds that the personality change "did not last much longer than about two to three years" (as reported in secondary summaries of his work) [23].
- Too high: theists accept the datum and deny the step to "no God". Nancey Murphy (CUP 2006) holds that neuroscience shows the capacities once given to the soul are brain processes, and places survival in God's recreation of the body [24]. Swinburne (OUP 2013) holds that a soul can depend on the brain for its functioning in this life without being the brain [25].
- Too high, but weaker than it looks: 24% of philosophy-of-mind specialists choose "dualism" on consciousness (n=95) [3], and 37% of neuroscientists in the 2026 PNAS survey agreed with an item describing two interacting realities [13]. Neither isolates the rival this line needs, a soul that uses the brain and can exist without it. The PhilPapers "dualism" option includes property dualists such as Chalmers, who deny a separable soul. In the PNAS survey, 64% of the same respondents also endorsed reductive physicalism, so many ticked both, and the response rate was 1.39%.

**Rule applied (the file's version: no soul, so no natural afterlife, so most religions lose a foundation):** Start at 5.
- (a) holds: mind-brain dependence is mainstream neuroscience (+1).
- (b) does not hold: theists accept the datum, but the ones who grant it any force (Christian physicalists such as Murphy and van Inwagen [26]) grant that it counts against the soul, not against God. Dualists such as Swinburne grant neither. I found no named leading theist who grants that it is evidence against God.
- (c) holds: Draper's SEP entry presents it as a live argument for local atheism (+1).
- (d) does not hold, or cannot be checked: the needed premise, that most religions need a surviving person, is true of Christianity, Islam and Hindu and Buddhist rebirth. No survey shows it to be a minority view among philosophers of religion.
- (e) does not hold: the rival that blocks the argument is substance dualism, on which a soul depends on the brain while embodied but can exist without it. No survey isolates how many specialists hold it. The 24% PhilPapers "dualism" figure includes property dualists, who agree there is no separable soul, and the PNAS item is ambiguous (see above). Named defenders exist (Swinburne), but nothing shows "many" in the sense used for for-05's (e), where the rival, physicalism, is held by 60% of specialists.
- (f) holds: getting from "no soul" to "no God" needs the contested step that God could not or would not raise or recreate the person, which Christian physicalists deny (−1).

Total: 5 +1 +1 −1 = **6**.

Draper's direct version (theism makes close dependence less expected) concludes against God without the soul step, so (f) would not hold and the line would be 7. Scored on the same "best version" basis, for-05 would rise to 6 or 7, so the two would move together.

**Proposed:** change 5 to 6. Strongest reason: the datum is about as solid as neuroscience gets, an SEP author presses it against God, and no survey shows a sizeable share of specialists holding the soul-preserving rival. The only discount is the contested step from "no soul" to "no God", since theists route around it through resurrection. Confidence 55.

**Text fixes:**
- Body, Gage sentence: add the recovery. Replace "Phineas Gage took an iron rod through his frontal lobe in 1848 and his friends said he was no longer Gage." with:
  > "Phineas Gage took an iron rod through his frontal lobe in 1848 and his friends said he was no longer Gage, though historians have since found that he recovered enough to work for several years as a stagecoach driver in Chile."
- best_reply overstates: not every brain injury "produces a different person". Replace with (274 chars):
  > "The radio analogy predicts that damage would garble the signal, not change the station: some brain injuries do not just impair a person but change their values and character, and a tumour's removal can reverse the change, which is what you expect if the brain is the person."
- Body, "A growing number of Christian philosophers simply concede the point" has no source for "growing". Replace with:
  > "A number of Christian philosophers, such as Peter van Inwagen and Nancey Murphy, simply concede the point"
- Body, "which many New Testament scholars argue is what the earliest texts taught". Replace with:
  > "which New Testament scholars such as Joel Green (already in the file's sources) argue is what the earliest texts taught"
- Status (237 chars; the current text is accurate but rhetorical, so this is optional):
  > "The dependence of mental life on the brain is among the best-established findings in neuroscience; theists either accept it and locate survival in resurrection rather than a soul, or argue that dependence shows correlation, not identity."

**If the owner disagrees:** he would have to count the generic 24% "dualism" figure, or the ambiguous PNAS item, as "many specialists" accepting a soul that survives the brain. That turns (e) on and returns the line to 5.

## Double counting

**for-05 vs against-07: distinct data, keep both.** for-05's datum is that phenomenal consciousness exists and resists physical explanation. against-07's datum is that its contents, personality and unity depend closely on the brain. Draper treats them as two separate pieces of evidence pulling opposite ways in one ledger: existence favours theism, dependence favours physicalism [11, §6.2]. Mackie does the same [4, pp. 130-131]. Counting both is correct, and it is how the specialist literature counts them. The double count is in the text, not the scores: for-05's best_objection and body use against-07's datum as the naturalist reply. The text fixes above remove that, so each datum is counted once.

Merge arithmetic, if the owner still wants one line. The two lines sit on opposite sides, so a merge would remove one line from each column, or fold one into the other:
- Current numbers (42/42). Remove both: 37/74 = 50%. Fold for-05 into against-07 (for-05 removed): 37/79 = 46.8%. Fold against-07 into for-05 (against-07 removed): 42/79 = 53.2%.
- Proposed numbers (for-04 = 6, for-05 = 5, against-07 = 6, so 43/43). Remove both: 38/75 = 50.7%. Fold for-05 into against-07: 38/81 = 46.9%. Fold against-07 into for-05: 43/80 = 53.8%.

A one-sided fold moves the headline by about 3 points for structural reasons alone, because the ratio loses 5 or 6 from one column. Draper's verdict ("far from clear") supports a net close to zero. Keeping both lines, at 5 and 6, gives a net of 1 against, so no merge is recommended.

**for-04:** none of the listed pairs touch it. Two adjacent overlaps are worth knowing about. Evolutionary debunking of moral beliefs (Street [9]) is a natural-explanation argument close to against-03, but it is not scored in for-04's strength, so nothing is counted twice. "Binding obligation in a world of particles" also appears as a worldview consistency criterion. That is a separate scoring system, not the evidence columns.

**Headline with all proposals:** for 42 + 1 = 43, against 42 + 1 = 43. 43/86 = 50%, unchanged. If only for-04 moves: 43/85 = 50.6%. If only against-07 moves: 42/85 = 49.4%.

## Rule problems (not scored)

1. **(d) and (e) often fire on the same disagreement.** For for-04, "God is needed to ground moral facts" being a minority premise (d) and "godless realism is an adequate rival" (e) are one fact seen from two sides. The same happens in for-05 with non-physicalism as a minority view (d) and physicalism as an adequate rival (e). Lines whose rival is a majority view get −2 for one disagreement. Suggested rewording: "(e) a rival explanation of the datum, *other than the denial of the premise counted under (d)*, is accepted as adequate by many specialists."
2. **"Best version" versus "the line as written".** For for-05 and against-07 the score depends on which version is scored. The file versions give 5 and 6. The best published likelihood versions (Page, Cutter and Crummett; Draper) give 6-7 and 7. Suggested rewording: "Score the version stated in the line's summary; if a stronger published version uses different premises, score that instead and rewrite the summary to match."
3. **(f) is unclear for arguments that already end in a personal being.** "A moral lawgiver" and "a designer" are personal, so the rule's example ("a cause" to "a personal God") does not apply, but neither is yet the God of any religion. Suggested rewording: "(f) the argument's conclusion falls short of a personal, good creator (for), or of the absence of any such being (against)."
4. **(c) has no bar for "treats as live".** Some arguments have their own SEP entry (moral arguments). Others appear only as a paragraph in another entry (consciousness, mind-brain dependence, both in Draper's atheism entry). Suggested rewording: "(c) an SEP entry, or an Oxford or Cambridge handbook chapter, *devoted to the argument* presents it as live," with a paragraph-mention counting for half or not at all.
5. **(b) is asymmetric for against-lines that work through the afterlife.** Theists readily grant that brain-dependence counts against the *soul*. The rule asks whether they grant it counts against *God*, which almost no theist will, because the line's own text says the step from soul to God is contested. That makes (b) and (f) hard to pass together. Suggested rewording: "(b) ... grant that the datum is at least some evidence for this side's conclusion *or for the intermediate claim the line relies on*."
6. **"Many specialists" has no threshold, and surveys rarely isolate the rival that matters.** In for-05, 34% deny a hard problem and 60% are physicalists, so (e) clearly holds. In against-07, the generic 24% "dualism" figure mixes property dualists, who agree with the line, with substance dualists, who reject it, so I could not show "many". Suggested rewording: "many = at least 25% in the best available survey of the field, or several named leading specialists where no survey exists."
