# Explanation and history under the two new rule texts: line-by-line re-check

Researcher model: Claude Fable 5.1. Date: 2026-10-28. Read-only on the repo; nothing edited.

Rules tested (RUBRIC-FIXES.md, sections 1 and 3):

- **Explanation.** "An answer names a cause, a mechanism or a necessity that the tradition's own best statement still asserts; a refusal to answer, however principled, is not one, and an inherited cosmology counts only where the tradition still asserts it. Add 1 more, once, if any of its answers is treated by philosophers across the divide as a distinctive resource."
- **History, early text.** "An early, stable text, fixed within two generations of the founder or founding events and transmitted without substantive change since (for a tradition with no founder, the oldest stratum of its canon if stably transmitted)."

Result in one line: the expected effects hold. Buddhism loses the cosmos point (explanation 7→6, total 29→28); Judaism loses the early-text point (history 5→4, total 25→24). The other 26 explanation answers, all five remaining bonuses and the other five early-text rulings survive the new wording, three of them with caveats recorded under Contested calls. A generation is taken as 25 to 30 years, so "two generations" is about 60 years.

Checks that were asked for and came out "still asserted":

- **Hinduism's cyclic creation.** Not merely inherited. The Gita, the text every Hindu school accepts, says at 9.7-8 that at the end of a kalpa all beings enter the Lord's prakriti and at the next beginning he sends them forth again, presiding over prakriti; the Brahma Sutra opens (1.1.2) by defining Brahman as that from which the origin, subsistence and dissolution of this world proceed [10][11]. That names a cause (Brahman or Ishvara) and a mechanism (prakriti under his presidency). Modern teachers from Radhakrishnan to the Swami Mukundananda commentary fetched here still state it as doctrine [10]. Asserted.
- **"The soul" in the three theisms.** The rule requires a cause, a mechanism or a necessity, not all three. All three theisms name a cause: the Catechism says "every spiritual soul is created immediately by God, it is not produced by the parents" and is "the form of the body" (CCC 365-366) [12]; the Quran has God breathe his spirit into the human being (32:9, 15:29), though 17:85 declines to describe the soul's nature [14]; Judaism's Saadia (Beliefs and Opinions VI) and Maimonides treat the soul as created with the body [18]. A named cause with a stated relation to the body is an answer of the same kind as naturalism's physicalism, which names a mechanism (brain processes) with no cause. Both count.
- **Naturalism's physicalism.** Papineau's SEP statement, that everything spatiotemporal is physical and mental states are physical states, names a mechanism and is the majority position among philosophers [20]. Asserted. Its cosmos answer stays a refusal: Carroll's 2018 paper says any account "must ultimately bottom out in a set of brute facts" [21]. Oppy's necessary-initial-state model is an alternative that would count as "a necessity" [22]; see Contested calls.
- **Deism's suffering answer.** The classic deist statement is Pope's Essay on Man IV: God is not "like some weak prince" who would "reverse his laws" for favourites, nor will "burning Etna, if a sage requires, forget to thunder"; partial evil is universal good because God acts by general not particular laws [25]. Paine's chapter on miracles adds that supposing the Almighty acts by suspensions of nature "is degrading the Almighty into the character of a showman" [26]. Flew's modern deism rejects Christianity "due to the problem of evil" and keeps an Aristotelian creator who does not intervene [27]. That names a mechanism (unsuspended general laws) and a reason for it (the creator's perfection), the same answer naturalism is credited for with a creator in front of it. Voltaire's Lisbon poem is the tradition's own refusal, but it is one voice against Pope, Tindal, Paine and Flew. Asserted, confidence 55.
- **Islam's suffering answer.** Q 2:155-157 and 67:2 state suffering as trial; Rouzati's 2018 survey confirms this remains the mainstream reading, while noting the Quran gives no systematic theodicy [14][15]. Asserted.
- **Judaism's suffering answer.** The Yigdal, sung at every weekday morning service, states Maimonides' eleventh principle: God rewards the kind according to their deeds and brings evil on the wicked according to their wickedness [17]. Soloveitchik's Kol Dodi Dofek refuses the why-question [19], but the liturgical and Maimonidean statement is still asserted daily. Asserted.

The bonuses, re-checked against "philosophers across the divide":

- **Naturalism (Draper).** Theists van Inwagen (Gifford Lectures 2006), Otte (2000) and Plantinga treat Draper's argument from the distribution of pain and pleasure as the evidential argument they must answer [23][24]. Holds.
- **Buddhism (diagnosis of suffering).** Parfit's appendix J, Flanagan's naturalised Buddhism and Thompson's critical but respectful treatment are all non-Buddhist [8][9]. Holds.
- **Islam (kalam).** The SEP cosmological-argument entry names al-Ghazali and Ibn Sina for the two families; Craig (Christian) defends and Oppy and Morriston (atheists) attack the kalam form under its Arabic name [16]. Holds, under Alex's 2026-10-28 ruling that originators keep the credit.
- **Hinduism (karma).** Weber, Herman, Reichenbach, Kaufman, Chadha and Trakakis, none of them Hindu, debate karma as a systematic theodicy [30]. Holds.
- **Christianity (the suffering God).** This is the thinnest. Zizek, a self-described materialist atheist, builds The Puppet and the Dwarf (2003) and The Monstrosity of Christ (2009) on the claim that Christianity is distinctive because God himself is abandoned on the cross, and that this is a resource no other religion offers [28]; Hans Jonas, a Jewish philosopher, proposes a suffering, non-omnipotent God after Auschwitz in explicit conversation with the Christian idea [29]. Those are philosophers across the divide treating the answer as distinctive, but neither treats it as an answer to the problem of evil in the way van Inwagen treats Draper. Holds, confidence 55; the alternative witness is Nagel's concession in Mind and Cosmos that theism would account for consciousness and intelligibility, which is a bonus for theism in general, not for Christianity.

## Changed lines

### Buddhism, explanation: 7 → 6

**Evidence.**
- The Buddha lists "the cosmos is eternal", "not eternal", "finite", "infinite" among the questions "undeclared by me" because they are "not connected with the goal" and do not lead to awakening, with the simile of the man who refuses treatment until he knows who shot the arrow (MN 63) [1]. On the beginning: "From an inconstruable beginning comes transmigration. A beginning point is not evident" (SN 15.3) [2].
- What the early texts do assert about lawfulness is that it is a standing fact: "whether or not there is the arising of Tathagatas, this property stands, this regularity of the Dhamma, this this/that conditionality" (SN 12.20) [3]. That asserts the explanandum, not a cause or ground of it, and is the same shape as Carroll's brute fact, which the naturalism file scores as a refusal [21].
- The inherited cosmology (Aggañña Sutta; Abhidharmakosa ch. 3: Mount Meru, world-systems that expand and contract) is what the file currently credits [6]. The Dalai Lama writes that "Buddhism must abandon many aspects of the Abhidharma cosmology", and treats the cosmology as "secondary to the account of the nature and origins of sentient beings" (quoted in Lopez, Buddhism and Science, pp. 69-70) [4]; in The Universe in a Single Atom he keeps a beginningless universe with no creator and declines a first cause [5]. The retained part names no cause for lawfulness; the discarded part is the cosmology the file credited.
- Across the divide the point is conceded by Buddhism's own sympathetic readers: Thompson grants the practical core and denies that the tradition offers a cosmology that competes with science [9]; the file's own body text already calls this "silence".

**Rule applied.** Start at 2. Answers that the tradition's best statement still asserts: minds, a beginningless stream of consciousness not reducible to matter, which the Dalai Lama and the Abhidharma both state (plus 1); obligation, karma and Santideva's argument from no-self to compassion (plus 1); suffering, the four truths (plus 1). A lawful cosmos: the Buddha set the question aside, a first point is declared undiscoverable, and the inherited world-system cosmology is disowned by the tradition's own teachers, so no point. Distinctive resource, once, for the diagnosis of suffering (plus 1). Six.

**Proposed.** 7 → 6, confidence 65. The remaining 35 is the reading under Contested calls 1, on which the retained cycle-and-karma cosmology counts as a mechanism; that reading would also credit naturalism's Big Bang cosmology and so would not change the gap between the two.

**Ready-to-paste `why`.**

Start at 2. Answers minds, through a beginningless stream of consciousness that its Abhidharma analyses and its teachers still assert; obligation, through karma and compassion grounded in no-self; suffering, its central diagnosis: plus 3. On a lawful cosmos it refuses: the Buddha set the question aside in MN 63, a first point of the cycle is declared undiscoverable, and the world-system cosmology its texts inherited is disowned by the Dalai Lama, so no point. The diagnosis of suffering is treated across the divide as a distinctive resource: plus 1. Six.

**Sources to add.** "Donald S. Lopez Jr., Buddhism and Science: A Guide for the Perplexed (2008)"; "Dalai Lama XIV, The Universe in a Single Atom (2005)". MN 63 is a primary text and can be named in the `why` without a sources entry, as other files do with suttas.

**Body text.** The third paragraph of buddhism.md ("on these questions the score credits only the cyclic cosmology it inherited, as every tradition's inherited cosmology is credited") describes the old rule and must change with the score; a one-clause replacement: "on these questions the score counts its silence as silence."

### Judaism, history: 5 → 4

**Evidence.**
- Final form of the Torah: the standard range is the Persian period, c. 450-350 BCE, with the Greek translation (c. 250 BCE) as the latest possible date and a minority dating the final redaction to the Hellenistic period; Römer's dating essay puts the earliest secure dating of Pentateuchal texts at 350-300 BCE [36]. The founder and founding events the text narrates (Moses, Exodus, Sinai) are set in the 13th century BCE; the tradition's own count is longer. The gap is at least 800 years, about 25 generations.
- Stability: Qumran attests three text-types side by side (proto-MT, pre-Samaritan, the Septuagint's Hebrew source) from the 3rd century BCE to the 1st century CE; Tov dates the uniformity of the proto-Masoretic text to "the end of the 1st century CE" and calls it accidental rather than the product of a standardisation [37]. Substantive variants include Deut 32:8 ("sons of God" against "sons of Israel"), on which textual critics agree the Qumran reading is older [38], and the two editions of Jeremiah. The text is stable from about 100 CE, not from its fixing.
- In favour of the point: the Judean Desert texts outside Qumran "display almost complete identity with the medieval tradition of MT" (Tov) [37], and the Ketef Hinnom amulets carry the priestly blessing in the 7th century BCE [39]. The first shows two thousand years of stability after the 1st century, which the new rule does not ask about; the second attests three verses, not a text.
- Calibration: under the new rule Christianity's credited text is within one generation of its founder and Islam's within two; Hinduism's is credited on the no-founder clause. Judaism's file names Moses as its founder and charges the late biography on that basis, so the no-founder clause is not open to it without changing the founder line too (Contested calls 3).

**Rule applied.** Start at 5. Holds: a continuous, documented community of 2,500 years (plus 1). The Torah reached its present form some eight centuries after the events it narrates and was textually plural until the first century CE, so it is not an early, stable text under the rule: no point. The kingdoms are attested from the ninth century BCE, so the Kings narrative draws no deduction. Gone against: the Exodus and conquest without support (minus 1); Moses and the patriarchs known only from late texts with legend (minus 1). Four.

**Proposed.** 5 → 4, confidence 70. Total 25 → 24.

Gone against: the Exodus and conquest lack archaeological support (minus 1); Moses and the patriarchs are known only from late texts with legend (minus 1). Four.

## Contested calls

1. **Does Buddhism's retained cosmology count as a mechanism?** The Dalai Lama discards Mount Meru but keeps a beginningless universe of world-systems shaped by the karma of beings [4][5]; SN 12.20 asserts conditionality as a fixed element [3]. If "a mechanism" is read as any account of how the cosmos runs, that counts, and Buddhism keeps 7. But on that reading naturalism's Big Bang cosmology and physical law count too (the naturalism report's calibration point 4), and naturalism goes to 7. The rule's author intends brute fact to stay a refusal, so "a lawful cosmos" must mean the ground of lawfulness, which neither tradition names. The two should move together; this report moves neither up and Buddhism down.
2. **Naturalism's cosmos: Carroll or Oppy?** Oppy argues naturalism is preferable to theism even on a necessary initial state and treats the initial state as necessary [22]. That is "a necessity" in the rule's words, and Leftow has built a "naturalist cosmological argument" from it. If Alex scores Oppy as naturalism's best statement, naturalism's explanation is 7 and its total 31. This report keeps Carroll because the file lists both and the brute-fact position is the one the file's body text already states; Alex should decide which naturalist speaks for the tradition.
3. **Does Judaism have a founder?** The file names Moses and charges late biography on that basis. If Alex instead treats rabbinic Judaism as founderless, the no-founder clause applies, and the question becomes whether the Torah's oldest stratum is stably transmitted. The archaic poems (Exodus 15, Judges 5) and the Ketef Hinnom blessing are early but are embedded in a text that was plural until c. 100 CE, and the parallel case, the Rigveda, is a complete recension with a dated fixing. The point would be arguable but weak; and the founder line would then have to be re-scored (no late-biography deduction, history back to 5).
4. **Buddhism's early fixing.** Wynne (2004), Gombrich and Analayo argue from the texts' formulaic structure and cross-recension agreement that a fixed oral text existed within the first generations [44][48]; Schopen argues nothing about the canon's contents is certain before the 5th-century commentaries [45]; Frauwallner puts the Vinaya's Skandhaka at least 150 years after the Buddha [46]; the first dated external evidence is Ashoka's list of seven named texts c. 250 BCE, about five generations after [47]. The new rule credits a text fixed within two generations; the evidence for Buddhism is inference, where for Paul and Uthman it is dated documents. If Alex accepts the inference, Buddhism's history goes 6→7 (total 29, or 28 after the explanation change here). The alternative rule text in RUBRIC-FIXES section 3 ("stable since first fixing") would give the point without this argument. Orality then stops being charged twice.
5. **Islam under Shoemaker's dating.** If the text was fixed under Abd al-Malik (c. 685-705), the gap is 53 to 73 years, at or just past two generations. The majority position (Sinai, van Putten, Déroche) keeps it inside, and Fudge's 2025 review finds Shoemaker's case weak [34][35]. Noted so the ruling does not depend on a date Alex has not seen contested.
6. **Christianity's bonus.** Zizek and Jonas are the across-the-divide witnesses; both treat the suffering God as distinctive, neither as a theodicy. If Alex wants the bonus to require that the answer be used as an answer by the other side, Christianity's drops and its explanation is 6; Nagel's concession about theism and consciousness would then be the fallback, and it would have to be shared with Islam and Judaism.
