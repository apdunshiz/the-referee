# Islam: score re-examination

## Summary

Current total 22/50 (history 5, consistency 5, explanation 6, testability 1, moral 5). Proposed total 25/50 (history 6, consistency 5, explanation 7, testability 2, moral 5).

The research confirms the file's two hardest judgments: the biographical sources are late and legendary (every camp agrees, from Donner to Shoemaker), and the manuscript record now supports an early fixed text better than the revisionists of the 1970s expected (Sadeghi and Goudarzi 2012, Sinai 2014, van Putten 2019). The revisionist line (Crone, Shoemaker) has not displaced that; Fudge's 2025 review article in BSOAS treats Shoemaker's late-dating as methodologically weak, and even Shoemaker accepts a historical Muhammad attested by non-Muslim sources within a decade of his death. What the research changes is calibration. Three facts that the rules credit or debit for other traditions were not applied the same way to Islam: a continuous documented community (credited to Christianity, Judaism and Hinduism, not to Islam); a distinctive philosophical resource (the kalam cosmological argument and the Avicennan contingency argument are the two cosmological arguments the Stanford Encyclopedia names Islamic thinkers for, yet Islam got no bonus while Christianity, Buddhism, Hinduism and Naturalism did); and an exposed central claim (the "uncorrupted" half of Islam's core claim could have been refuted by the Sanaa palimpsest and was not, which parallels the way Christianity's resurrection exposure is scored). On consistency and moral the counts stay, but the items named should change: abrogation is a texts-and-law matter the rule excludes, and the uncreated Quran belongs in its place; and the third moral deduction is slavery sanctioned from its texts, the same fact that costs Christianity, rather than the status of women, which Christianity's texts share and are not debited for.

## History

**Current:** 5, confidence 60. "Start at 5. Foundations that hold: a well-attested founder (plus 1); a text fixed within about a generation and transmitted with unusual stability, the Birmingham folios supporting an early written text while the Sanaa palimpsest shows a variant also circulated (plus 1). Gone against: biographical traditions written down over a century later with much that historians treat as legend (minus 1); accounts of earlier prophets that conflict with the record where one exists (minus 1). Five."

**Evidence:**
- Founder attested outside the tradition within a decade: Hoyland treats Thomas the Presbyter (c. 640) as the first explicit non-Muslim reference to Muhammad and says the account "inspires confidence that it ultimately derives from first-hand knowledge"; the Doctrina Jacobi (634) is earlier but garbled [1]. Shoemaker's dissent (Death of a Prophet, 2012) is about the death date (632 vs. alive in 634-35), not existence [2]. Founder +1 holds in every camp.
- Early stable text: the Sanaa palimpsest parchment dates 578-669 CE at 95% (Sadeghi and Bergmann), its lower text is a non-Uthmanic companion-type codex with the same content and ordering of material, and Sadeghi argues the standard text's wording is older than the lower layer [3][4]. Birmingham folios: 568-645 CE at 95.4% [5]. Van Putten (BSOAS 2019) shows 14 early manuscripts share the same arbitrary spelling of niʿmat Allāh at the same positions, which he argues proves a single written archetype standardised "not long after the reign of ʿUthmān" [6]. Sinai (BSOAS 2014, two parts) weighs the Marwanid-closure case and concludes closure before c. 685 is more probable, while conceding some parts could have been corrected to c. 700 [7]. Text +1 holds; the Déroche reservation about radiocarbon and the Shoemaker late-dating are minority positions.
- The revisionist case: Shoemaker's Creating the Qur'an (2022) dates the fixed text to ʿAbd al-Malik's reign, c. 70 years after Muhammad [8]. Fudge's review article (BSOAS 2025) finds it built largely on secondary sources, misreading Welch and cherry-picking Robinson and Donner, and says the field has no new consensus [9]. Donner's Believers thesis (2010) is about the early community's ecumenical self-description, not about the text's lateness; Crone's objection to it was that its only direct evidence is a handful of Quranic verses [10]. Neither moves the text or founder credits.
- Continuous, documented community: not credited in the file. PERF 558, a bilingual Arabic-Greek tax receipt dated 22 AH / 643 CE, is the oldest dated document of the Islamic era; the Dome of the Rock inscription (691-2) carries Quranic text and the shahada [11]. Christianity, Judaism and Hinduism each receive +1 for a continuous documented community; Islam's is the best documented of the four from its first decade.
- Late biography: Ibn Ishaq's sira survives only in Ibn Hisham's redaction (d. 833), and the hadith canon is ninth-century; Donner, Brown and Shoemaker all treat much of it as legend. −1 holds and matches the gospels deduction for Christianity.
- Conflict with the record: Haman as Pharaoh's official (Q 28, 40) is what Lammens called "the most glaring anachronism"; Silverstein (JSAI 2008) explains it as a literary rather than historical link and the defence is a minority of Muslim scholars [12]. Q 4:157 on the crucifixion is read as a denial by the long consensus of Muslim and non-Muslim readers; Lawson (2009) and Reynolds read it as anti-Jewish polemic rather than historical denial, which is contested [13]. −1 holds for the same reason Judaism takes −1 for Exodus: the Quran's claim is that these accounts are God's own words, which makes them foundational in a way the gospel infancy narratives are not for Christianity.

**Rule applied:** Start at 5. Holds: founder (+1), early stable text (+1), continuous documented community (+1). Gone against: late biography (−1), accounts that conflict with the record (−1). Six.

**Proposed:** 5→6. The community credit is the same fact scored +1 for three other traditions. Confidence 70.

**If the owner disagrees:** he would have to say why a community documented from 643 CE counts for less than Christianity's, which is first documented by Pliny and Tacitus about 80 years after its founding.

## Consistency

**Current:** 5, confidence 55. "Start at 8. Three core doctrines need careful definition: divine determination alongside human responsibility, debated by its own schools for centuries; abrogation, where later verses override earlier ones; and a God named the Compassionate and Merciful alongside eternal punishment. Five."

**Evidence:**
- Qadar: the Ash'ari doctrine of kasb (acquisition) holds that God creates every act and humans "acquire" it; the SEP entry on al-Ghazali states that "a purely occasionalist model finds it difficult to explain how God can make humans responsible for their own actions" and that for him "humans have only the impression of a free will" [14]. Qadar is the sixth article of the Sunni creed (hadith of Gabriel), so it is core in a way Christian predestination is not in the Nicene creed; that asymmetry justifies debiting Islam and not Christianity.
- Mercy with eternal punishment: the same tension costs Christianity a point. Within Islam, Ibn Taymiyya's Fanāʾ al-nār and Ibn al-Qayyim's deliberations argued the Fire's duration depends on God's will; Hoover (Muslim World 2009) calls this "the most forthright challenge to the alleged consensus" in medieval Islam, and al-Subki refuted it [15]. The doctrine needs the same careful definition as in Christianity. −1 holds.
- Uncreated Quran: the Mu'tazila argued that if the Quran is God's literal speech, "he logically must have preceded his own speech"; the Ash'ari answer separates God's eternal inner speech (kalām nafsī) from the created recitation and Arabic utterance [16]. Wolfson (Philosophy of the Kalam, 1976) treats this as the Islamic counterpart of the Logos problem [17]. The Mihna (833-848) split the tradition over it. This is a core doctrine needing careful definition on the tradition's own best statement, exactly as the incarnation is for Christianity. It is not in the file.
- Abrogation: the rule says "disagreements between texts are a history matter, not scored here." Abrogation is a legal-hermeneutical principle for reconciling verses; its scope is disputed from about 20 (Suyuti) to 5 (Shah Wali Allah) [18]. The theological residue (does eternal speech change its mind?) was settled early by holding that the ruling's duration was always known. Christianity's supersession of the Mosaic law is the same kind of fact and is not debited. It should be dropped.
- Islam has no Trinity-type doctrine; its monotheism is structurally simpler, which the body text already says.

**Rule applied:** Start at 8. Needs careful definition: qadar with responsibility (−1), eternal speech in a created book (−1), mercy with eternal punishment (−1). Abrogation is a texts matter, not scored. Five.

**Proposed:** keep 5, with the named items changed (uncreated Quran in, abrogation out). Confidence 60.

**If the owner disagrees:** if he thinks qadar should not count because Christianity's Augustine-Calvin predestination problem was not debited, the score goes to 6; the fact to weigh is that qadar is creedal in Islam and predestination is not creedal in Christianity.

## Explanation

**Current:** 6, confidence 55. "Start at 2. Answers all four: a lawful cosmos and moral obligation through a creator and judge, minds through the soul, suffering as a test with recompense in the next life, including for children and animals: plus 4. None of its answers is treated across the divide as a distinctive resource. Six."

**Evidence:**
- The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy's cosmological-argument entry names Islamic thinkers for both main families: the temporal argument was developed by the mutakallimūn ("al-Ghāzāli (1058-1111) argued that everything that begins to exist requires a cause of its beginning") and "Arabic philosophers (falasifa), such as Ibn Sina (c. 980-1037), developed the argument from contingency, which was taken up by Thomas Aquinas" [19].
- Across the divide: the kalam argument is defended by Craig (a Christian who took the name and the form from al-Ghazali) and attacked by Oppy and Morriston (atheists) in the same entry; it is the most-discussed theistic argument in current philosophy of religion [19][20]. That is the definition of "treated by philosophers across the divide as a distinctive resource."
- Avicenna's metaphysics "had a great impact on Thomas Aquinas (and on medieval Western philosophy in general)" and "every Latin medieval philosopher from the late 12th century onwards encountered" it (SEP, Ibn Sina's metaphysics) [21].
- Ghazali's occasionalism is a principal subject of the SEP entry on occasionalism, which traces the view from him to Malebranche; it is also the point where Islam's answer to the "lawful cosmos" question is weakest, since lawfulness becomes God's habit rather than nature's [22][14].
- Against the bonus: the argument is an instrument of natural theology used by every theism, and Craig deploys it for Christianity. The Deism file says the cosmological arguments it "draws on are arguments, credited under explanation" yet gives Deism no bonus. But Deism borrows them; Islam produced them. The rule's bonus is for an answer "treated as a distinctive resource," and the kalam argument carries Islam's name in every textbook.
- The four answers hold as the file states; Islam's account of animal and infant suffering (recompense, Q and hadith on children of believers) is at least as developed as Christianity's, which the Christianity file calls weak.

**Rule applied:** Start at 2. Four answers (+4). The kalam cosmological argument, its answer to the lawful cosmos, is treated across the divide as a distinctive resource (+1). Seven.

**Proposed:** 6→7. Confidence 60.

**If the owner disagrees:** he would have to hold that an argument counts as the resource of whoever uses it, not whoever produced it; then Christianity's suffering bonus should also be examined, since the God-who-suffers answer is used by philosophers who are not Christians only as a Christian idea.

## Testability

**Current:** 1, confidence 55. "Exposure 2: the Quran's divine origin is argued from its literary inimitability, which no one who disputes the premise can test. Its early text is a foundation claim scored under history, not again here; claims that it anticipates modern science belong to popular presentation and are not scored. Failed, minus 1: prayer trials, counted once against every tradition that promises answered prayer. One."

**Evidence:**
- Exposure: the core claim has two halves, "God's word" and "uncorrupted." The second is exposed to manuscripts. The Sanaa palimpsest was the live chance to refute it: a seventh-century non-Uthmanic codex. It showed a text with the same content and variants of the companion-codex type, not a different Quran [3][4]. The Quran itself invites a check (Q 4:82, "had it been from other than God they would have found in it much discrepancy"). Christianity's exposure of 4 rests on the fact that a body could have refuted the resurrection; Judaism's 3 on a covenant that could fail visibly. Islam's preservation claim could have failed visibly and did not. Exposure 3 is the calibrated value. The rule's bar on double-counting applies to the held/failed kinds, not to the exposure number, otherwise Christianity's 4 would be barred too.
- Held, contested: Q 30:2-4 predicts a Roman recovery "within a few years" after a defeat. The Byzantine-Sasanian war is independently attested. The sura is Meccan on the standard dating (Nöldeke, Neuwirth, Sinai), which puts the prediction before Heraclius's victories of 622-628. Tesei (Der Islam 2018) argues it is vaticinium ex eventu with a terminus post quem of 628-29 [23]; Ghaffar replies that "a few years" is needlessly vague for an after-the-fact prophecy. A majority-dated, falsifiable, fulfilled prediction is a held kind; the dating dispute is why confidence is low.
- Failed, contested: the Quran repeatedly says the Hour is near (Q 54:1, 21:1, 33:63) and hadith have Muhammad say he and the Hour were sent "like these two fingers." Shoemaker (Apocalypse of Empire, 2018) and Donner both read the first community as expecting an imminent end [24][10]. Christianity takes −1 for "the apparent prediction of a return within a generation"; the Quran's statements are vaguer (no "this generation") but of the same kind. If the owner counts one he should count both.
- Prayer trials: −1, same as every tradition promising answered prayer. Holds.
- Science-in-scripture: correctly excluded as popular claims.

**Rule applied:** Exposure 3. Held: Rome prophecy (+1). Failed: prayer trials (−1), imminent Hour (−1). Two.

**Proposed:** 1→2. The strongest reason is the exposure of the preservation claim, which was tested by the palimpsest and held. Confidence 45. The Rome and Hour items cancel at equal confidence; if the owner rejects both, the result is still 2.

**If the owner disagrees:** he would have to say why the Sanaa palimpsest was not a real chance to refute Islam's core claim, when a tomb with a body is counted as a real chance to refute Christianity's.

## Moral

**Current:** 5, confidence 55. "Start at 8: obligatory charity, equality before God and hospitality extend to outsiders in its best expression. Wars and conquest in its name (minus 1); legal coercion of apostates and dissenters under traditional law (minus 1); the subordinate legal status of women written into traditional law (minus 1), each contested by reformers reading the same sources, which the rule does not restore. Five."

**Evidence:**
- Extends to outsiders: Q 5:8 (justice even toward a people you hate), Q 60:8 (kindness and fairness to non-hostile unbelievers), Q 49:13 (peoples and tribes made "that you may know one another"), zakat and the Constitution of Medina. Start at 8 holds.
- Wars and conquest: the conquests of 632-750 were carried out in its name; Donner's own thesis that they were an eschatological Believers' movement does not change that [10]. −1 holds.
- Persecution and coercion: all four Sunni schools prescribe death for male apostasy (Hanafis confine women instead), with repentance offered first in three; al-Alwani, Kamali and Ramadan argue the Quran itself has no temporal penalty [25]. Dhimmi status (Q 9:29, the Pact of Umar) made non-Muslims "second-class subjects, to be sure," in Mark Cohen's words, though protected and often lightly enforced [26]. −1 holds and is the same class as the inquisitions.
- Exclusion written into its structure: Jonathan Brown (Slavery and Islam, 2019), a believing scholar, writes that "the permissibility of slavery and concubinage is undeniable in the Qur'an" and that no classical scholar called for its prohibition [27]; Kecia Ali shows marriage law itself was built on the ownership idiom of slavery [28]. Saudi Arabia abolished slavery in 1962, Mauritania in 1981. This is exactly the fact that costs Christianity its third point.
- The file's third item, women's legal status, is written into the text (Q 4:11 half inheritance, Q 2:282 testimony, Q 4:34 guardianship). But 1 Tim 2:12, 1 Cor 11 and Eph 5 are also written into Christianity's texts and coverture into Christian states' law, and Christianity is not debited for it. Scoring it for Islam alone is a calibration error; scoring slavery instead gives the same −1 on the same fact as Christianity.
- Reform from within (Abduh, Kamali, Brown's own book) does not restore a point, as the rule says.

**Rule applied:** Start at 8. Wars and conquest in its name (−1); legal coercion of apostates and dhimmis (−1); slavery sanctioned from its texts, abolished in Muslim states only in the twentieth century (−1). Five.

**Proposed:** keep 5, with the third item changed from women's status to slavery. Confidence 70.

**If the owner disagrees:** if he wants women's legal status to count as a structural exclusion, Christianity (and Judaism, whose halakha also halves women's testimony and inheritance) must take the same deduction.

## Calibration check

1. **Continuous documented community.** Christianity: "a continuous, documented community (plus 1)." Judaism: "a continuous, documented community of 2,500 years (plus 1)." Hinduism: "a continuous, documented community for three millennia (plus 1)." Islam: no mention. Islam's community is dated from PERF 558 (643 CE) and the Dome of the Rock (691-2). Reconcile by crediting Islam +1.
2. **Structural exclusion.** Christianity: "slavery sanctioned from its texts for centuries (minus 1)." Islam: "the subordinate legal status of women written into traditional law (minus 1)." Same rule, different fact chosen; the women's fact exists in both traditions' texts and is debited only for Islam, while the slavery fact exists in both and is debited only for Christianity. Reconcile by naming slavery for Islam; the number does not change.
3. **Distinctive resource.** Naturalism gets +1 for "its prediction of the actual texture of suffering"; Buddhism for "the diagnosis of suffering"; Hinduism for "karma's completeness." Islam gets none, though the SEP cosmological-argument entry names al-Ghazali and Ibn Sina for the two main argument families. Reconcile by crediting Islam +1.
4. **Failed prediction.** Christianity: "the apparent prediction of a return within a generation" (−1). Islam: the Quran's "the Hour has drawn near" and the two-fingers hadith are not scored. Reconcile either by debiting Islam (as proposed) or by dropping Christianity's, which would move Christianity to 3.

## Rule problems (not scored)

- **Abrogation versus supersession.** The consistency rule excludes "disagreements between texts" but the file scored abrogation, a device for reconciling texts, as a doctrine. Christianity's setting aside of Mosaic law is the same shape and was not scored. The rule needs a line saying whether a tradition's own mechanism for ranking its texts is a doctrine or a texts matter.
- **State power is debited, not credited.** The moral rule's three classes almost guarantee −3 for any tradition that governed empires (Christianity, Islam) and −1 or −2 for traditions that mostly did not (Judaism, Buddhism). Judaism's file says so openly: "as a minority faith for most of its history it has little record of persecuting others." Islam governed from its first decade. Example: dhimmi law and the inquisitions each cost one point; the Jewish state's record before 586 BCE costs the same one point as the Arab conquests.
- **Exposure and foundation overlap.** Testability's exposure number and history's foundation credit both reward Christianity for the resurrection claim (history +1 for the event and the appearance claims; exposure 4 for the event). The rule should state that this is intended, so that the same double credit is applied to Islam's preservation claim (history +1 for the early text; exposure for the palimpsest test).
- **"Conflict with the record" falls harder on scriptures that claim to be God's own words.** Islam takes −1 for Haman and Q 4:157; Christianity takes nothing for the Quirinius census or the Exodus it inherits. The rule is defensible (the Quran's status makes its history foundational) but should say so.
