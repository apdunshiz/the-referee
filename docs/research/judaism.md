# Judaism: re-examination of the five scores

## Summary

Current total 28 (history 6, consistency 7, explanation 5, testability 3, moral 7). Proposed total 25 (6, 6, 6, 2, 5).

The research confirms the history 6 but not its arithmetic: the kingdom inscriptions (Tel Dan, Mesha, Assyrian annals) are not a founding event of the religion, and the file never credits the Torah as an early, stable text, which Hinduism and Islam were credited for. Two kinds of fact offset and the number stands. The research changes three lines. Explanation goes up, because Job is one voice, not the tradition's answer: Saadia, the Talmud and Maimonides give the same kind of answer to suffering (test, recompense in the world to come, evil as privation) that earned Islam its point. Testability goes down, because "survival of the people" is the continuous community already credited under history, and the rule forbids scoring a foundation claim twice. Moral goes down most, because the file's claim of "little record of persecuting others" and "no exclusion written into its structure" fails on the same kinds of fact that cost Christianity (slavery sanctioned from Leviticus 25) and Islam (the legal subordination of women): the Hasmonean state imposed circumcision and crucified dissenters, the Jewish kingdom of Himyar massacred Najran's Christians, and the modern state kept its Arab citizens under military rule for eighteen years. Consistency loses a point only if the owner keeps Islam's deduction for foreknowledge against freedom; the Mishnah and Maimonides state the same tension in so many words. All five proposals are evidence for the owner, not verdicts; the confidence figures say how far each should be trusted.

## Historical footing

**Current:** 6, confidence 65. "Start at 5. Foundations that hold: a continuous, documented community of 2,500 years (plus 1); the kingdom of Israel attested from the ninth century BCE by the Tel Dan and Mesha stelae and Judah from the eighth by Assyrian annals (plus 1). Gone against: the patriarchs, Exodus and conquest have little or no archaeological support in the form the texts describe (minus 1). Six."

**Evidence:**
- Finkelstein and Silberman find no trace of a Semitic migration across Sinai, find Jericho and Ai unoccupied at the time of their supposed conquest, and read the highland villages that appear around 1200 BCE as Canaanite pastoralists settling down, not invaders [1]. Dever, who otherwise attacks the book, agrees that Israel emerged largely from within Canaan [1].
- The Merneptah Stele (c. 1208 BCE) names "Israel" as a people already in Canaan, the earliest reference to Israel anywhere [8]. It is evidence for an early Israel and against a thirteenth-century Exodus on the biblical scale.
- Hoffmeier (Oxford, 1996) and Kitchen (2003) argue that the Egyptian place names, titles and customs in Exodus reflect real New Kingdom knowledge and that a small group leaving Egypt would leave no trace; Kitchen calls The Bible Unearthed "imaginative fiction" [1, 2, 3]. Friedman (2017) takes the middle position now common among critical scholars: a small Exodus of Levites, the only Israelite group with Egyptian names, later merged into Israel [4]. Even the best defenders defend a small event, not the text's.
- Tel Dan (1993–94) reads "House of David" and is accepted by most epigraphers; Davies's "place name" reading is a minority [5]. Mesha line 31 is disputed: Finkelstein, Na'aman and Römer (2019) propose "Balak," Lemaire and Delorme (2022) and Langlois defend "House of David" [6, 7]. Ahab at Qarqar (853), Jehu on the Black Obelisk (841) and Hezekiah in Sennacherib's annals are not disputed [9]. From the ninth century on, Kings matches the record.
- The Torah's final form is dated by most scholars to the Persian period, c. 450–350 BCE, with the Greek translation (c. 250 BCE) as the latest possible date; the classical Documentary Hypothesis has lost consensus to supplementary and fragmentary models, though Baden and Friedman defend a revised four-source version [10]. The text is late relative to Moses but early in absolute terms: the Ketef Hinnom amulets carry the priestly blessing from the seventh century BCE [12], and Tov finds the Judean Desert scrolls outside Qumran "almost completely identical" to the medieval Masoretic consonantal text, a stable stream for two thousand years [11].
- Within Judaism, the Exodus is treated as non-literal by Reform and much of Conservative Judaism; Wolpe's 2001 Passover sermon said so from a Conservative pulpit and drew Orthodox condemnation [13].

**Rule applied:** Start at 5. Holds: a continuous, documented community (+1); an early, stable text, complete by the Persian period and transmitted without change since, the credit Hinduism's Vedas and Islam's Quran received (+1). The kingdom inscriptions are not a founding event of Judaism (Sinai is); they are the reason the Kings narrative does not draw a "conflicts with the record" deduction of its own. Gone against: founding events without support, the Exodus and conquest (−1); the founder and patriarchs known only from texts written centuries later with legend, the kind charged to Buddhism and Islam (−1). Six.

**Proposed:** keep 6. Confidence 55. The number holds because two credits and two debits that the file does not name cancel; the owner should at least re-word the `why`. A stricter reading that also charges "claims that conflict with the record" (Jericho, Ai, Israel in Canaan by 1208) gives 5; the Hinduism-style reading that treats the founding stories as myth-by-the-tradition's-own-account gives 7.

**If the owner disagrees:** whether a text completed c. 400 BCE counts as "early" when the events it narrates are set eight centuries before it.

## Internal consistency

**Current:** 7, confidence 60. "Start at 8. One tension needs careful definition: a universal creator who makes a covenant with one particular people. Seven."

**Evidence:**
- Election is a real tension, not a slur: Novak's The Election of Israel (Cambridge, 1995) spends a book on how a universal God's choice of one people is to be stated, against Spinoza's inversion that Israel elected God [20]. The file's one deduction is justified.
- Pirkei Avot 3:15: "Everything is foreseen, yet freedom of choice is granted" [18]. Maimonides, Laws of Repentance 5:5, calls the question "longer than the earth and broader than the sea" and concludes that "we do not have the potential to conceive how the Holy One knows" while insisting that a person's actions are in his own hands [19]. Omniscience is one of his thirteen principles and free choice he calls a pillar of the Torah; the tradition's own best statement says the two cannot be reconciled by us. This is the kind of fact for which Islam lost a point ("divine determination alongside human responsibility, debated by its own schools for centuries").
- Judaism avoids the third Abrahamic charge: Gehinnom lasts at most twelve months for most sinners (Rosh Hashanah 17a), so "mercy alongside eternal punishment" does not apply [40].
- Corporate guilt (Exodus 20:5) against individual responsibility (Ezekiel 18) is a disagreement between texts and belongs to history, not here.

**Rule applied:** Start at 8. Needs careful definition: universal creator with a particular covenant (−1); foreknowledge with free choice, stated as beyond comprehension by Akiva and Maimonides (−1). Six.

**Proposed:** 7→6. Confidence 50. The strongest reason is parity with Islam: the same tension, charged there, is stated even more explicitly here. The low confidence is because Christianity, which has the same tension (Romans 9, Augustine, Calvin), was not charged; the owner may prefer to remove Islam's deduction instead and keep 7.

**If the owner disagrees:** whether Maimonides's "we cannot conceive how" is a careful definition that avoids contradiction or an admission that none is available.

## Explanatory reach

**Current:** 5, confidence 60. "Start at 2. Answers a lawful cosmos and moral obligation through a creator and lawgiver, and minds through the soul: plus 3. On suffering, Job declines to explain and offers God instead, which is an honest refusal, not an answer. No distinctive-resource bonus. Five."

**Evidence:**
- Harris's St Andrews Encyclopaedia entry (2024) surveys the tradition: the Mishnah holds suffering is punishment for sin; the Talmud rejects that as a universal and adds later reward in the World to Come and "sufferings of love" (Berakhot 5a), read as soul-making or divine intimacy; Saadia offers a test theodicy, a punishment theodicy and suffering as discipline, with the World to Come settling the accounts; Maimonides gives a free-will theodicy, locates evil in matter as privation (Guide III.10–12), and makes individual providence depend on intellectual perfection (III.17–18); Lurianic Kabbalah traces evil to the tzimtzum and the breaking of the vessels [14, 15, 16].
- Saadia's test-and-recompense answer is the same answer that earned Islam its fourth point ("suffering as a test with recompense in the next life").
- Job is one voice, and the tradition reads him in several ways: Maimonides treats him as a parable in which Job erred intellectually, not morally [15]; Avot 4:15 ("it is not in our hands to explain the security of the wicked or the afflictions of the righteous") is a confessed limit, not the system's whole account [18].
- Post-Holocaust thought splits: Berkovits keeps a free-will theodicy through hester panim; Rubenstein, Fackenheim and the "antitheodicy" Braiterman names (Soloveitchik, Levinas, Greenberg) refuse to justify God and ask instead "what am I supposed to do if confronted with evil" [14, 17]. These are the refusals the file took for the whole; they are the twentieth-century minority of a tradition that answered for two thousand years.
- Distinctive resource: Levinas's "useless suffering" and the protest tradition are cited across the divide, but as refusals, and the rule awards the bonus to an answer. No Jewish answer to suffering is singled out by non-Jewish philosophers the way the suffering God, karma or the Buddhist diagnosis are.

**Rule applied:** Start at 2. Answers a lawful cosmos and obligation through a creator and lawgiver, minds through the soul, and suffering through test, recompense in the world to come and evil as privation (+4). No distinctive-resource bonus. Six.

**Proposed:** 5→6. Confidence 70. The strongest reason: the rule asks whether the worldview gives an answer, and Judaism's medieval and rabbinic mainstream gives the same answers Islam was credited for; Job's silence is the body note's choice, not the tradition's best statement.

**If the owner disagrees:** whether a tradition that has several answers and no agreed one "gives an answer" in the rule's sense; note that Hinduism, with the same plurality, was credited.

## Testable claims

**Current:** 3, confidence 60. "Exposure 3: a permanent covenant could have failed visibly. Held, plus 1: the people's survival and return to the land, a promise rather than a foundation claim. Failed, minus 1: prayer trials, counted once against every tradition that promises answered prayer. Three."

**Evidence:**
- The rule: "foundation claims already scored under history are not scored again." The people's survival is the "continuous, documented community of 2,500 years" that history already credited (+1). Scoring it here is a double count.
- The return promise, Deuteronomy 30:1–5, is conditional ("and you return to the Eternal your God... then the Eternal will restore your fortunes") and undated [22]. Deuteronomy's final form is exilic or later [10], so the 538 BCE return is contemporaneous with the text, not a prediction it made. The 1948 return was secular in leadership and preceded no national repentance; the condition the verse states was not met.
- The tradition's own authorities did not read 1948 as fulfilment. The Three Oaths (Ketubot 111a) forbid a mass return before the Messiah, and Ravitzky's standard study shows the leading Haredi and Hasidic authorities of the day treating Zionism as "forcing the end" [23]. A promise its own custodians denied was being kept is not a held prediction.
- Failed kind not counted: Ezekiel 26:7–14 has Nebuchadnezzar destroy Tyre and leave it never rebuilt; the thirteen-year siege (586–573) failed to take the island, and Ezekiel 29:17–20 concedes that "he got no reward" from Tyre and reassigns Egypt as his wages. Egypt was not made a forty-year desolation either. Udd (Tyndale Bulletin, 2005) and the Bulletin for Biblical Research article "Dissonant Prophecy in Ezekiel 26 and 29" (2000) both treat it as a dated prediction that the book itself revises [24]. This is the same kind of fact as the "return within a generation" charged to Christianity (Ehrman, 1999) [25]; Christianity was not charged for Ezekiel either.
- Prayer: STEP (Benson et al., 2006, 1,800 bypass patients) found no benefit from intercessory prayer and more complications in those who knew they were prayed for [21]. Judaism promises answered petition (Deuteronomy 4:7; the Amidah's "heal us"; Mi Sheberach for the sick), so the once-per-tradition deduction applies as it does to Christianity, Islam and Hinduism. The file applies it the same way. Note STEP's intercessors were Christian; the application to Judaism is by kind.
- Exposure 3 is defensible against Christianity's 4 and Islam's 2: extinction would have refuted a permanent covenant, but "the Messiah has not yet come" can never fail.

**Rule applied:** Exposure 3. Held: none that is not a foundation claim or a conditional, undated promise. Failed: prayer trials (−1). Two. If the Ezekiel prophecy is charged as the Olivet discourse was for Christianity (−1), one.

**Proposed:** 3→2. Confidence 55. The strongest reason: the survival credit is the history credit scored twice, which the rule forbids in so many words. The second deduction is left to the owner because it requires choosing between charging both Abrahamic heirs of Ezekiel or neither.

**If the owner disagrees:** whether the 1948 return counts as a held promise when the texts set a condition and the tradition's own leading authorities denied the fulfilment.

## Moral fruit

**Current:** 7, confidence 65. "Start at 8: the prophetic ethic of justice for the poor and the stranger extends to outsiders. Wars and conquest commanded in its own texts and carried out by its ancient states (minus 1); as a minority faith for most of its history it has little record of persecuting others, and no exclusion is written into its structure. Seven."

**Evidence:**
- Persecution and coercion by states built on it. Hyrcanus "subdued all the Idumeans; and permitted them to stay in that country, if they would circumcise their genitals, and make use of the laws of the Jews" (Josephus, Antiquities 13.257) [26], after destroying the Samaritan temple on Gerizim, confirmed by Magen's excavation [29]. Weitzman (HTR, 1999) reads the circumcisions as forced for political reasons; Cohen (1999) softens it to a "simplified account" of annexation on condition of conformity; neither denies the condition [27, 28]. Alexander Jannaeus crucified 800 Pharisee opponents (Antiquities 13.380) [26]. The Jewish kingdom of Himyar under Yusuf (Dhu Nuwas) killed the Christians of Najran in 523, attested by three Sabaic inscriptions and the Syriac letters; Bowersock (Oxford, 2013) rejects the view that Himyar was only quasi-Jewish, and modern historians read the massacre as political repression as much as religious [30, 31].
- The modern state. Israel kept over ninety per cent of its Arab citizens under military government from 1948 to 1966, with curfews, land confiscation and movement permits (Robinson, Stanford, 2013) [37]; Morris's archival study documents expulsions in 1948 alongside flight [38]; B'Tselem's 2021 report describes one regime of differentiated rights between the river and the sea, a characterisation the Israeli government rejects [39]. Whether Israel is a "state built on" Judaism is contestable (its founders were secular), but the rule's own calibration counts the Soviet Union as built on naturalism, a looser link than a state whose Law of Return, rabbinical courts and 2018 Basic Law are defined by Jewish identity.
- Exclusion written into its structure. Leviticus 25:44–46: foreign slaves "shall become your property... for them to inherit as property for all time" [32]; Hezser (Oxford, 2005) shows Jews kept slaves throughout the Hellenistic and Roman periods, and the Shulchan Aruch (Yoreh Deah 267) still codifies the laws of the Canaanite slave [33]. These are the verses that cost Christianity its third point.
- Women: Mishnah Yevamot 14:1, "the man divorces his wife only willingly; the woman is divorced whether she is willing or unwilling" [34], the root of the agunah problem; women are barred from most testimony (Hauptman's survey in the Rabbinical Assembly responsa) [35]; and in Israel the 1953 Rabbinical Courts Jurisdiction Law makes these rules state law for every Jewish marriage [36]. This is the kind of fact that cost Islam its third point ("the subordinate legal status of women written into traditional law").
- Non-Jews: Katz's Exclusiveness and Tolerance documents Talmudic civil-law distinctions between Jew and gentile and the medieval work (Meiri's "nations bounded by religion") needed to exempt Christians and Muslims from them [41]. Opposition from within does not restore a point.
- In its favour: the rule starts at 8 for an ethic that extends to outsiders, and the prophetic command to love the stranger, repeated 36 times, plainly qualifies; the Hebrew slave law was softened by the rabbis ("whoever buys a Hebrew slave buys himself a master"); and the whole record of states built on Judaism is about 80 years of Hasmonean rule, a century of Himyar and 78 years of Israel.

**Rule applied:** Start at 8. Wars and conquest in its name, the biblical command and the Hasmonean and modern wars (−1); persecution or legal coercion of dissenters and a subordinate group, the imposed circumcision, the 800 crucified, Najran, military government 1948–66 (−1); exclusion written into its structure, permanent chattel slavery of foreigners sanctioned from Leviticus 25 and the legal subordination of women codified in halakha and enforced by state law (−1). Five.

**Proposed:** 7→5. Confidence 60. The strongest reason: the rule counts kinds of fact "present in the record," not their share of the record, and Christianity and Islam each lost a point for a kind of fact that is present in Judaism's texts and states in the same form. A 6 is defensible only if the owner treats the Hasmoneans and Himyar as too thin a record and Israel as not built on the tradition, in which case the slavery and women's status line alone still costs a point.

**If the owner disagrees:** whether eighteen centuries without state power excuse a tradition from the record its states did make. The rule, as written and as applied to naturalism's seventy years of Soviet rule, says it does not.

## Calibration check

1. **Slavery from the same verses.** Christianity: "slavery sanctioned from its texts for centuries (minus 1)." Judaism: "no exclusion is written into its structure." The sanctioning text in both cases is Leviticus 25:44–46, and Hezser shows Jewish slaveholding continued through antiquity [32, 33]. Reconcile by charging Judaism; the alternative, dropping Christianity's charge, would require the owner to say Leviticus is not a Christian text.

2. **Women's legal status.** Islam: "the subordinate legal status of women written into traditional law (minus 1)." Judaism: silent, though Yevamot 14:1, the testimony rules and the 1953 law are the same kind of fact, and the Israeli case is a state enforcing them today [34, 35, 36]. Reconcile by charging Judaism or un-charging Islam; charging only one is the inconsistency.

3. **States built on it.** Naturalism: "wars and conquest by states built on it, the Soviet and Maoist regimes (minus 1); persecution of dissenters and believers by those states (minus 1)." Buddhism: "persecution of minorities by Buddhist movements and states in Myanmar and Sri Lanka (minus 1)." Judaism: "little record of persecuting others." The Hasmonean, Himyarite and Israeli records are documented in the same sense [26, 30, 37]. Reconcile by charging Judaism; if the owner instead narrows "states built on it," the naturalism deductions go first.

Two further cases the owner should see: the early-text credit (Hinduism +1 for orally transmitted Vedas, Buddhism −1 for orally transmitted suttas, Judaism neither), and the myth exemption (Hinduism's stories "treated as myth in much of its own tradition" are "not a failed claim," while the Exodus, treated as myth by Reform and much of Conservative Judaism, is charged).

## Rule problems (not scored)

- **Presence without duration.** The moral rule counts a class of fact as present whether a tradition's states lasted 80 years or 1,500. It punishes traditions that held state power longer and, applied honestly, erases Judaism's main moral distinction, which is that it has done little harm for eighteen centuries because it could do none.
- **"States built on it" is undefined.** The Soviet Union counts for naturalism; whether Israel counts for Judaism, or the Hasmoneans for a religion that had not yet become rabbinic, is a judgement call the rule does not make. Example: the same 1948–66 military government is a deduction or not depending on that call.
- **The conquest is counted as fiction and as record.** History deducts because the conquest of Canaan has no archaeological support; moral deducts because the conquest was "carried out by its ancient states." One event cannot be both. The texts' command is real and suffices, but the `why` should not cite the event.
- **Prayer trials by analogy.** STEP tested Christian intercessors praying to a Christian God. Charging Hinduism and Judaism on its result is a transfer by kind that the rule asserts rather than argues.
- **The distinctive-resource bonus rewards fame.** Four of seven worldviews get it; it tends to go to whichever answer has a literature, not to whichever answer is better. Example: Maimonides's privation account is Augustine's, but only Christianity's suffering God is treated as a resource.
- **"Early" is undefined.** Early relative to the founder (Islam), early in absolute terms (Hinduism), or early relative to the events narrated (where the Torah fails)? The history line cannot be applied evenly until the rule says which.
