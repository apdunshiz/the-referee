# Naturalism (no God): score re-examination

Worldview file: `content/worldviews/naturalism.md`. Rules: `content/site.json`, `criteria`. Research done 2026-10-28.

## Summary

**Current total 30/50. Proposed total 29/50** (history 6, consistency 6→5, explanation 5→6, testability 7→6, moral 6).

The research confirms the shape of the current scores: naturalism's footing in the scientific record, the null results it has banked (prayer trials, psi, near-death perception) and the state-atheist record of the USSR, Albania, China and Cambodia are all well documented, and the file reports them fairly. It changes three lines, two down and one up, because the rules were applied to naturalism a little more generously than to the others on two points and a little more harshly on one. (1) Testability credits "mind tracking brain" after history already credited "the scientific record of ... the brain"; the rule's no-double-count clause, enforced on Islam and Christianity, bars that. (2) Consistency counts two strained doctrines but not the third that Nagel and Plantinga put on the table: how truth-tracking reason arises from fitness-tracking selection. Nagel's book treats consciousness, cognition and value as three separate problems; the file counts two. (3) Explanation gives Christianity, Islam and Judaism a point for "minds through the soul" but gives naturalism nothing for "minds are what brains do", although physicalism is the majority view among philosophers and is at least as much an answer. The "states built on it" clause is already applied to naturalism on the moral line and the evidence supports both deductions, so moral stays at 6.

## Historical footing

**Current:** 6, confidence 70. *"Start at 5. It rests on no founding events; its one foundation is the scientific record of cosmic history, evolution and the brain, which holds under scrutiny (plus 1). Nothing it rests on has failed. Six."*

**Evidence:**
- Ontological naturalism, in the standard reference statement, is the claim that everything spatiotemporal is physical or constituted by the physical, motivated by the causal closure of physics; a thesis that rests on the sciences, not a tradition with a founder, text or community (Papineau, SEP, rev. 2020) [1].
- The record it rests on (cosmic age and scale, common descent, dependence of mental function on brain) is undisputed; the other six files treat it as the background every tradition must accommodate.
- Against a higher score: no early text, no founder, no community in the rule's sense. Deism, with none of these, scores 5. Organised humanism (ethical societies from the 1870s onward) is a documented lineage, but crediting it as a "community" would oblige crediting deism's eighteenth-century societies too.
- Against a lower score: the one historical claim popular atheism makes that scrutiny has gone against, the "conflict thesis" of perpetual war between science and religion, is a popular claim and excluded by the rule, as science-in-scripture is excluded for Islam and Hinduism. The core claim "religions are human creations" has a research programme behind it (cognitive science of religion), but theists accept natural mechanisms of belief as compatible with theism (Plantinga 2011) [2], so it neither holds nor fails as history.

**Rule applied:** Start at 5. Holds: the scientific record the worldview rests on (+1). No founder, text or community to credit. Nothing gone against. Six.

**Proposed:** keep 6. Confidence 70.

**If the owner disagrees:** he would have to count organised humanism as a continuous documented community, and then do the same for deism.

## Internal consistency

**Current:** 6, confidence 65. *"Start at 8. Two core matters need careful definition: how conscious experience arises in a world of particles, and how binding moral obligation does. Six."*

**Evidence:**
- Consciousness: Chalmers (1995) [3] set the hard problem; Frankish's illusionism (2016) [4], the most thoroughgoing physicalist reply, concedes the appearance of phenomenal consciousness must itself be explained away. Papineau: most philosophers think physicalism can answer the anti-physicalist arguments "though a significant minority disagrees" [1]. The deduction stands.
- Obligation: Mackie's queerness argument (1977) [5] and Street's Darwinian dilemma (2006) [6] are naturalist arguments that objective obligation does not fit a world of particles; Wielenberg's *Robust Ethics* (2014) [7] answers by positing non-natural, causally inert moral facts, a careful definition of exactly the kind the rule deducts for. The deduction stands.
- A third strain: Plantinga's evolutionary argument against naturalism holds that unguided selection tracks fitness, not truth, so a naturalist has a defeater for the faculties that produced naturalism. The replies (Fitelson and Sober 1998 [8]; Ramsey, Fodor, Fales and others in Beilby's *Naturalism Defeated?*, 2002) turn on carefully defining how belief content can be causal and selected for; Post's review finds naturalism "has resources the argument has not refuted" but does not call the argument defeated [9].
- Nagel's *Mind and Cosmos* (OUP 2012) [10], by a non-theist, treats this as a distinct problem: its central chapters are "Consciousness", "Cognition" and "Value", on the ground that an evolutionary account of why we hold beliefs is not an account of why they are true. Sober and Leiter-Weisberg [11] reject his conclusion; neither denies the three are different questions.
- Against a third deduction: the majority view is that the EAAN fails, and "mind in a world of particles" can be read as one doctrine with two aspects. The rule's example list names obligation once, not twice.
- Not counted here (see Calibration): determinism alongside responsibility, which the Islam file deducts and no other file does.

**Rule applied:** Start at 8. Needs careful definition: conscious experience in a world of particles (−1); binding obligation in a world of particles (−1); truth-tracking reason from fitness-tracking selection (−1). Five.

**Proposed:** change 6→5. Strongest reason: naturalism's own sharpest non-theist critic lists cognition as a third problem separate from consciousness, and the best naturalist replies to Plantinga are "careful definitions to avoid contradiction" in the rule's sense. Confidence 55.

**If the owner disagrees:** he would have to hold that the reliability of reason is a sub-case of the consciousness problem; Chalmers' "easy problems" include cognition, so that reading has support.

## Explanatory reach

**Current:** 5, confidence 65. *"Start at 2. Answers suffering (an indifferent nature explains it completely) and moral obligation (secular realism): plus 2. Leaves a lawful cosmos and conscious minds to brute fact or future science. Its prediction of the actual texture of suffering is treated across the divide as a distinctive resource: plus 1. Five."*

**Evidence:**
- Suffering: Draper's "Pain and Pleasure" (*Noûs* 1989) [12] argues the biological distribution of pain and pleasure is far more probable on the hypothesis of indifference than on theism. Theists treat it as the evidential argument they must answer (Howard-Snyder 1994, Otte 2000, van Inwagen's Giffords 2006 [13]). Both the answer and the distinctive-resource bonus are earned.
- Obligation: naturalism gives several answers (Cornell realism and moral functionalism, which Papineau reports most moral philosophers now favour [1]; Wielenberg's robust realism [7]). It also contains error theory, which dissolves the question. The rule credits "an answer", not agreement, and the strain is already charged under consistency. The point is earned, though the file's "secular realism" should cite the naturalist realists, since Wielenberg's facts are explicitly non-natural.
- Minds: the file says naturalism leaves minds to "brute fact or future science". Its best statements say otherwise: physicalism (51.9% of philosophers accept or lean to it, 32% non-physicalism, 2020 PhilPapers survey [14]) says minds are physical processes; functionalism and illusionism say what kind. Contested, but Christianity's "a soul made in God's image", Islam's and Judaism's "the soul" are credited as answers and are no less contested and no more detailed.
- Lawful cosmos: Carroll's best statement is that explanation "must ultimately bottom out in a set of brute facts; the universe simply is", with laws as Humean patterns rather than governors (2018) [15]. That is the rule's "honest refusal", as Job is for Judaism. Oppy's alternative, a necessary initial state [16], is a real answer and the mirror of "God is necessary"; if the owner scores Oppy rather than Carroll, naturalism reaches 7. See Calibration on the Buddhist and Hindu cosmology credits.
- Against crediting minds: Chalmers [3] says physicalism explains function, not experience; Nagel [10] says it does not explain cognition either. But the rule scores whether an answer is given and charges its weakness under consistency, which it already does.

**Rule applied:** Start at 2. Answers: suffering (+1); moral obligation (+1); conscious minds, through physicalism (+1). Lawful cosmos: brute fact, an honest refusal (0). Distinctive resource, Draper's argument (+1). Six.

**Proposed:** change 5→6. Strongest reason: "minds are what brains do" is the same kind of answer as "minds are souls", and the three theisms get the point for it. Confidence 55.

**If the owner disagrees:** he would have to say why "the soul" answers the minds question and "a physical process" does not, or remove the soul point from Christianity, Islam and Judaism.

## Testable claims

**Current:** 7, confidence 70. *"Exposure 4: one confirmed miracle or one lawlike event needing a supernatural cause would refute it. Held claims, plus 1 each: no miracle confirmed to skeptical investigators; mind tracking brain in every detail; the prayer trials' null result. No failed kind. Seven."*

**Evidence:**
- Exposure: Carroll's *The Big Picture* (2016) says the "Core Theory" of everyday physics is complete and on its own terms rules out telekinesis, astrology and survival after death [17]; a demonstrated case of any would refute it. That is a real exposure, higher than Hinduism's or Islam's. Against 4: any anomaly can be absorbed as "an unknown natural cause", and Earman's *Hume's Abject Failure* (2000) [18] shows the standard "no testimony could establish a miracle" is not Bayesian-sound, so the exposure holds only if naturalists accept a reachable standard. Carroll's version does; 4 is defensible, 3 arguable.
- Held, prayer: STEP (Benson et al., *American Heart Journal* 2006), 1,802 bypass patients, no effect of intercessory prayer and more complications in the group that knew it was prayed for [19]. Counted once against every tradition that promises answered prayer; it is the mirror credit here.
- Held, no supernatural or paranormal effect under controlled conditions: Bem's precognition failed pre-registered replication (Ritchie, Wiseman, French, *PLoS ONE* 2012) [20]; AWARE (2014) placed hidden images at ceiling height in 15 hospitals across 2,060 arrests and no survivor identified one; AWARE II (2023, 567 arrests), 0 of 28 identified the visual target [21].
- Double count: "mind tracking brain in every detail" is the brain science history already credits as "the scientific record of cosmic history, evolution and the brain". The rule: "foundation claims already scored under history are not scored again". Islam's file applies it ("Its early text is a foundation claim scored under history, not again here"), as does Christianity's.
- Failed kinds: the strongest candidate is fine-tuning, which the SEP entry (Friederich, rev. 2021) [22] says naturalists answer with a multiverse or treat as a "primitive coincidence". Naturalism made no checkable prediction about the constants, so it is not a failed check; it is weighed under explanation. No failed kind.

**Rule applied:** Exposure 4. Held: no supernatural or paranormal effect under controlled conditions, with miracles, psi and near-death perception counted as one kind (+1); the prayer trials (+1). Mind-brain dependence: a foundation claim already scored under history, not scored again. No failed kind. Six.

**Proposed:** change 7→6. Strongest reason: the rule's own no-double-count clause, already enforced on Islam and Christianity. Confidence 60.

**If the owner disagrees:** he would have to treat brain-dependence as a prediction tested after the fact rather than part of the record naturalism rests on, and then let Islam's manuscript evidence count under testability too.

## Moral fruit

**Current:** 6, confidence 60. *"Start at 8: humanist ethics extends to every person. Wars and conquest by states built on it, the Soviet and Maoist regimes (minus 1); persecution of dissenters and believers by those states (minus 1); no exclusion written into its structure. Six."*

**Evidence:**
- Persecution: the 1918 RSFSR constitution, article 65, disenfranchised "servants of churches and religious cults"; the 1929 Law on Religious Associations stripped religious bodies of legal personality and confined clergy to registered localities [23]; the 1937–38 terror shot clergy by the thousands (176 in Tyumen region alone) [24]. Froese's *The Plot to Kill God* (UC Press 2008) [25] treats the campaign as an explicitly atheist project. Albania closed or demolished every church and mosque in 1967 and wrote "the state recognises no religion and supports atheist propaganda" into article 37 of its 1976 constitution [26]. The Khmer Rouge defrocked an estimated 40,000–60,000 monks, a regime memo claimed 90–95 percent eliminated, and Cham Muslims were killed for refusing pork [27]. Goossaert and Palmer call the Cultural Revolution "the most thorough destruction of all forms of religious life in Chinese and, perhaps, human history" (2011) [28]. The deduction is as well supported as any in the folder.
- Wars and conquest: no war has been fought "in the name of" atheism. The Soviet annexations (Baltics 1940, Afghanistan 1979) and China's 1950 occupation of Tibet were by states whose founding doctrine was dialectical materialism and whose constitutions enshrined atheism, so they fall under "states built on it". The Buddhist ("wars and nationalist violence in its name") and Hindu ("communal violence and conquest in its name by nationalist movements") deductions use the same loose standard, so it is applied evenly.
- Exclusion: the 1918 disenfranchisement is written into a state's constitution, but it is the same fact as the persecution already counted; charging it twice would be the double count the Islam file avoids with apostasy law. Humanist ethics, naturalism's best expression, has no exclusion.
- Against the deductions: Marxism-Leninism is a political religion with a materialist premise, not naturalism as philosophers state it, and states built on secular humanism rather than Marxism (Zuckerman's Denmark and Sweden, 2008 [29]) have no such record. But the rule scores any state built on the stance and does not restore points for better states, as it does not for Christianity or Islam.

**Rule applied:** Start at 8. Wars and conquest by states built on it (−1); persecution and legal coercion of believers by those states (−1); no exclusion beyond the one already counted (0). Six.

**Proposed:** keep 6. Confidence 55.

**If the owner disagrees:** he would have to decide that Marxist-Leninist states were not "built on" naturalism, which would also mean deciding that Hindutva movements are not "in the name of" Hinduism.

## Calibration check

1. **Testability double count.** Islam: *"Its early text is a foundation claim scored under history, not again here."* Naturalism: history credits *"the scientific record of cosmic history, evolution and the brain"* and testability credits *"mind tracking brain in every detail"*. Reconcile toward Islam's reading: drop the testability point (7→6).
2. **The minds question.** Christianity: *"minds through a soul made in God's image"* (+1); Judaism: *"minds through the soul: plus 3"*. Naturalism: *"Leaves a lawful cosmos and conscious minds to brute fact or future science"* (0). Physicalism is an answer of the same kind. Reconcile by crediting naturalism (5→6) or by removing the soul point from all three theisms.
3. **Determinism and responsibility.** Islam: *"divine determination alongside human responsibility, debated by its own schools for centuries"* (−1). Naturalism's physical determinism alongside moral responsibility, resolved by compatibilism, is the same kind of fact and is not deducted; neither is Calvinist predestination in the Christianity file. The smaller change is to the Islam line, so this report does not fold it into naturalism's score; if Islam's deduction stays, naturalism's consistency should be 4.
4. **Inherited cosmology.** Buddhism: *"a cosmos of cyclic world-systems, inherited and counted like everyone else's"* (+1); Hinduism: *"a lawful cosmos through cyclic creation"* (+1). Neither explains why there are laws; both are credited under the "inherited cosmology counts" clause. Naturalism's inherited cosmology is Big Bang cosmology and it is not credited. Either the clause applies to naturalism too (6→7) or the lawful-cosmos question is read strictly for everyone and the Buddhist and Hindu points come off.

## Rule problems (not scored)

- **"States built on it" has no test for "built on".** Marxist-Leninist states were built on dialectical materialism, one naturalism among many; Hindutva governments are built on a nationalism that uses Hinduism. Each scorer decides what counts, and the decision moves a point.
- **The moral rule scales with time in power.** Christianity and Islam held states for over a millennium, naturalism for seventy years. Deductions are per class, not per century, which softens this, but a stance that never held power (deism) is capped at 5 while one that held it briefly can reach 6.
- **Explanation credits any answer at all.** "The soul", "brute fact", "cyclic world-systems" and "a physical process" are all one-phrase answers, and whether each counts depends on the scorer's taste. A short list of what counts as an answer (a mechanism, a cause, or a stated necessity) would remove most of the Calibration inconsistencies.
- **Naturalism has no "own best statement".** The consistency and explanation rules judge "the tradition's own best statement", but naturalism has no creed. Rosenberg's nihilism answers obligation with "there is none"; Wielenberg answers with non-natural facts; the score moves a point depending on which naturalist is chosen. The file should name whose statement it scores (Oppy and Carroll are the sensible choices and are already in its sources).
- **Exposure of a universal negative.** "Nothing supernatural exists" is exposed to one counterexample in principle but shielded by "unknown natural cause" in practice; Earman's point about Hume [18] cuts both ways. The 0–5 exposure scale gives no guidance for claims of this shape.
