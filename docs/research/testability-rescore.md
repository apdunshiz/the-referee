# Testable claims: rescore of all seven under the new rule (draft for Alex, 2026-10-28)

The rule applied, verbatim from RUBRIC-FIXES.md section 2: exposure is the number of kinds of refuter the central claim admits, 0 to 5 (a historical finding; a controlled experiment; a public prediction with a date; an observation anyone can make today; a logical demonstration); then add 1 for each kind of checkable claim that has held and subtract 1 for each kind that has failed; each kind is scored once; foundation claims scored under history are not scored again; the prayer trials count once, as a failed kind for every tradition that promises answered prayer, never as a held kind; meditation and rebirth-memory research count the same way for every tradition that makes those claims.

Three working principles, applied the same way to all seven:

1. The central claim is the file's `core_claim`. A refuter kind counts if a finding of that kind could in principle show that claim false on the tradition's own best statement. A dated prediction counts only where the tradition's authoritative texts make one.
2. Any kind a tradition is charged or credited on under held/failed is admitted under exposure. You cannot fail a test your claim does not admit. This is why the controlled-experiment column is yes for every tradition that promises answered prayer.
3. A failed or held kind is counted once per tradition. A second failed dated prediction (Ezekiel for Christianity, which inherits him) does not subtract again.

Current scores: Naturalism 6, Buddhism 3, Christianity 2, Deism 2, Islam 2, Judaism 2, Hinduism 1.
Proposed: Naturalism 5, Buddhism 3, Christianity 2, Deism 4, Islam 3, Judaism 3, Hinduism 3.

Controlled experiment is yes for all seven because every religion here promises answered prayer or makes contemplative claims that have been trialled, and naturalism and deism are refuted by a positive result. Logical demonstration is yes for all seven because each central claim contains a doctrine philosophers have argued is incoherent (the consistency criterion already names them). The spread comes from the historical, dated-prediction and open-observation columns: a past event (the resurrection, the revelation, the Exodus) admits a historical finding but no present observation; a present-tense claim (no supernatural, craving causes suffering, a permanent covenant) admits an observation but, for naturalism and deism, no historical founding to dig up except the fossil record.

## Naturalism

**Evidence**
- Historical finding: the claim that everything arose by natural processes is routinely said to be falsifiable by a fossil out of sequence, Haldane's rabbit in the Precambrian; Godfrey-Smith notes one such find would first be checked for misidentification or hoax, and if genuine would show serious error somewhere in the package rather than overturn it at once [1]. That is still a refuter in principle, which is all exposure asks.
- Controlled experiment and open observation: Carroll's statement of the position says the Core Theory rules out telekinesis, astrology and survival after death; a demonstrated case of any refutes it [2]. Earman shows the Humean bar on miracle testimony is not Bayesian-sound, so a naturalist cannot in principle refuse historical or observational evidence [3].
- Logical demonstration: Plantinga's evolutionary argument against naturalism claims the view is self-defeating; Fitelson and Sober's reply is the standard answer, which shows the demonstration is attempted and contested, not out of reach [4].
- Held kind: Bem's precognition effect failed three pre-registered replications [5]; AWARE (2,060 arrests, 15 hospitals) and AWARE II (567 arrests) placed hidden visual targets and no survivor identified one [6]. One kind, no paranormal effect under controlled conditions, held.
- Prayer trials: STEP (1,802 bypass patients) found no effect of intercessory prayer and more complications in those who knew they were prayed for [7]; the Cochrane review of ten trials found the evidence equivocal [8]. Under the new rule this is a failed kind for traditions promising answered prayer and a held kind for no one, so the file's second held point goes.

**Rule applied:** Exposure 4: historical finding, controlled experiment, open observation, logical demonstration; no dated prediction. Held: no paranormal effect under controlled conditions (plus 1). Prayer trials: not credited. Mind tracking brain: scored under history. Failed: none. Five.

**Proposed:** 6 → 5. Confidence 55.

why: Exposure 4: a historical finding, a fossil out of sequence; a controlled experiment showing a paranormal effect; an observation open to anyone today, a public miracle; or a logical demonstration that it defeats itself would each refute it. It makes no dated prediction. Held, plus 1: no supernatural or paranormal effect confirmed under controlled conditions, in psi and near-death studies. Prayer trials count once, against traditions promising answered prayer, and credit no one. Mind tracking brain is scored under history. Five.

Sources to add: "Peter Godfrey-Smith, Theory and Reality (2003)"; "Stuart Ritchie, Richard Wiseman and Christopher French, Failing the Future, PLoS ONE 7 (2012)"

## Buddhism

**Evidence**
- Historical finding: the tradition's own best statement does not stake the teaching on its teacher; the suttas invite each hearer to verify (ehipassiko), and Siderits presents the doctrine as arguments that stand or fall on their own [9]. A finding that the Buddha did not exist would cost it under history, where the late biography is already charged, but would not refute the claim that craving produces suffering. No.
- Dated prediction: the Vinaya has the Buddha say the true dharma will last five hundred years because women were ordained, a thousand otherwise. Nattier shows the statement is absent from Mahasamghika texts and that modern scholars have generally been reluctant to accept it as the Buddha's words, and that Buddhaghosa's fifth-century commentaries replaced it with a five-thousand-year timetable [10]. Analayo treats the five-hundred-year figure as the canonical prediction that did not come true [11]. The authoritative text makes a dated prediction and the tradition's own commentators revised it after the date passed, the same shape as 2 Peter 3:4 for Christianity.
- Controlled experiment, held: Goyal's review of 47 trials found moderate evidence for anxiety and depression (d about 0.3 to 0.4) and no advantage over active treatments [12]; Van Dam and co-authors warn the field's claims outrun its methods [13]. Held in the weak form, the same credit Hinduism takes.
- Prayer: the Lotus Sutra promises deliverance from fire, flood and execution to those who call Avalokiteshvara's name [14]; Reader and Tanabe show this-worldly benefits lie at the heart of Japanese Buddhism including the established sects [15]. Failed kind, once.
- Logical demonstration: whether rebirth and obligation survive no-self is argued by Williams and answered by Siderits and Harris [16]; the demonstration is live.
- Rebirth memory: Stevenson and Tucker's cases rest on witness recall Tucker himself calls faulty in many cases [17]. Neutral for every tradition that makes the claim.

**Rule applied:** Exposure 4: controlled experiment, dated prediction, open observation, logical demonstration; no historical finding. Held: meditation research (plus 1). Failed: the five-hundred-year limit (minus 1); prayer trials (minus 1). Rebirth memory neutral. Three.

**Proposed:** keep 3. Confidence 60. The number is the same whether or not the Vinaya prediction is counted (exposure 3 with no deduction also gives three); the proposal counts it because the new rule names dated predictions as a kind and Christianity and Islam are charged on the same kind. This is a change to the file's held/failed list; new source: Nattier [10].

why: Exposure 4: a controlled experiment on meditation, the Vinaya's dated limit on the teaching, an observation open to anyone today of whether craving produces suffering, and a logical demonstration that rebirth without a self is incoherent each reach it; no historical finding does, since the teaching stands whoever taught it. Held, plus 1: meditation research partly supports its attention claims. Failed, minus 1 each: the five-hundred-year limit; prayer trials, once against every tradition promising answered prayer, as its devotional texts do. Three.

Sources to add: "Jan Nattier, Once Upon a Future Time: Studies in a Buddhist Prophecy of Decline (1991)"

## Christianity

**Evidence**
- Historical finding: the claim is a bodily resurrection in a named decade; a body, or a contemporary refutation, would have ended it, and Paul stakes the faith on it (1 Corinthians 15:14). Yes, the clearest case in the table.
- Dated prediction: Mark 9:1, 13:30, Matthew 10:23 and 1 Thessalonians 4:15 put the return within the first generation; Allison, a believing scholar, concludes most of what Jesus envisaged cannot be identified with past events [18]; Ehrman documents the later gospels softening the sayings [19]. Wright's preterist reading is answered by Adams from Mark's own usage [20], and it does not cover Paul. Hays's volume of Christian scholars accepts the problem and argues the prophecy was conditional [21]. Failed, on the majority critical reading, as the file has it.
- Open observation: no observation anyone can make today bears on a first-century event. The church's survival and spread (Matthew 16:18, 24:14) are the continuous community credited under history, which the file already refuses to score again. No.
- Controlled experiment: John 14:13-14 and James 5:15 promise answered prayer; STEP and the Cochrane review are the trials [7][8]. Failed, once.
- Logical demonstration: the Trinity and incarnation have a standing literature on coherence (Tuggy) [22]. Yes.
- Held kinds considered and not credited: the temple prediction (Mark 13:2) is neutral because Mark's date relative to 70 is unsettled; see Contested calls for its parallel with Islam's Rome prophecy.

**Rule applied:** Exposure 4: historical finding, controlled experiment, dated prediction, logical demonstration; no open observation. Held: none. Failed: the return within a generation (minus 1); prayer trials (minus 1). Two.

**Proposed:** keep 2. Confidence 55. The new rule does not move Christianity: its exposure was already counted on refuter kinds, and the two deductions stand. Whether it should be 3 turns on the open-observation cell or the temple prediction; both are in Contested calls.

why: Exposure 4: a historical finding, a body or a contemporary refutation, could have shown the resurrection false; a controlled experiment bears on its promise of answered prayer; its texts fix a date, the return within a generation; a logical demonstration could show the incarnation incoherent. No open observation reaches a past event. Church survival is scored under history. Held: none. Failed, minus 1 each: the return within a generation, on the majority critical reading; prayer trials, once against every tradition promising answered prayer. Two.

Sources to add: "Dale Allison, Jesus of Nazareth: Millenarian Prophet (1998)"; "Dale Tuggy, Trinity, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy (2024)"

## Deism

**Evidence**
- The central claim has two halves. The creator half admits only a logical demonstration: show the universe necessary, as Oppy's necessary-initial-state model attempts [23]; the file already says so.
- The no-intervention half is a negative universal, and Paine states it in exactly that form: the supernatural parts of scripture carry every mark of fraud, and petitionary prayer is absurd [24]. One reliable historical miracle (historical finding), one positive prayer trial (controlled experiment), or one public miracle (open observation) refutes it. The deism report made this point and set exposure 3; counting the kinds gives 4.
- Held: nothing. Alex's ruling stands: no miracle confirmed and the null prayer trials are absences, not claims deism staked and won, and the new rule bars the prayer trials as a held kind for anyone. Naturalism's one held kind (no paranormal effect under controlled conditions) is not claimed by deism, whose claim is about the creator acting, not about psi.
- Failed: the file scores none. The deism report proposed Paine's design-in-organisms as a failed kind answered by natural selection [24]; the file did not adopt it and it is left in Contested calls.
- Dated prediction: no authoritative text. No.

**Rule applied:** Exposure 4: historical finding, controlled experiment, open observation, logical demonstration; no dated prediction. Held: none, by ruling. Failed: none. Four.

**Proposed:** 2 → 4. Confidence 50. The whole move comes from counting the no-intervention half, which the old file's exposure number ignored. The one fact against: that half is the same claim Alex ruled earns no held credit, so Alex may prefer to limit exposure to the creator half (then 1; see Contested calls).

why: Exposure 4: a historical finding of reliable divine action, a controlled experiment showing an answered prayer, or an observation open to anyone today of a public miracle would refute the no-intervention half; a logical demonstration that the universe is necessary would refute the creator half; it makes no dated prediction. Nothing is credited as held: that no miracle has been confirmed and that the prayer trials were null are absences rather than claims it staked and won, and the prayer trials credit no tradition. No failed kind. Four.

Sources to add: none; Paine (1794) is already listed.

## Islam

**Evidence**
- Historical finding: the preservation claim was exposed to the Sanaa palimpsest, a seventh-century non-Uthmanic codex, and it showed companion-codex variants rather than a different Quran [25]. The early text itself is credited under history and is not scored again as held; exposure counts the kind.
- Dated prediction: Q 30:2-4 predicts a Roman recovery within a few years (bid' sinin, three to nine); Meccan on the standard dating, which puts it before Heraclius's victories; Tesei argues ex eventu after 628 [26]. The Quran also says the Hour is near (Q 54:1, 21:1, 33:63), and Shoemaker and Donner read the first community as expecting an imminent end [27]. The file has these cancel; kept.
- Open observation: the Quran issues a public challenge to produce a sura like it (Q 2:23) [28], but the file's judgment that no one who disputes the premise can run the test stands: there is no agreed standard by which an observation could count as a success. No, contested.
- Logical demonstration: Q 4:82 says that had it been from anyone other than God they would have found in it many contradictions [28]; the text names its own logical refuter. Yes.
- Controlled experiment: the Quran promises answered supplication (Q 2:186, 40:60); STEP and Cochrane are the trials [7][8]. Failed, once.

**Rule applied:** Exposure 4: historical finding, controlled experiment, dated prediction, logical demonstration; no open observation. Held and failed cancel: the Rome prophecy (plus 1) against the imminent Hour (minus 1). Failed: prayer trials (minus 1). Three.

**Proposed:** 2 → 3. Confidence 50. The gain is one exposure point for the dated-prediction and controlled-experiment kinds, which the old hand-set 3 did not count. If Alex drops both Rome and the Hour the result is still three.

why: Exposure 4: a historical finding, a different seventh-century Quran, could have refuted the preservation claim and the Sanaa palimpsest did not; a controlled experiment bears on its promise of answered prayer; its text makes dated predictions; a logical demonstration of discrepancy is a test the Quran itself invites. Inimitability has no test a doubter can run, so no observation reaches it. Held and failed cancel: the Rome prophecy against the imminent Hour. Failed, minus 1: prayer trials, once against every tradition promising answered prayer. Three.

Sources to add: "Tommaso Tesei, The Romans Will Win! Q 30:2-7 in Light of 7th c. Political Eschatology, Der Islam 95 (2018)"

## Judaism

**Evidence**
- Historical finding: the claim that God revealed himself in history is exposed to the archaeological record of the Exodus and conquest, which Finkelstein and Silberman and most field archaeologists find unsupported in the form the texts describe [29]. Already charged under history; counts as a refuter kind here.
- Dated prediction: Ezekiel 26:7-14 has Nebuchadnezzar destroy Tyre, never to be rebuilt; the thirteen-year siege failed to take the island and Ezekiel 29:17-20 concedes he got no reward and reassigns Egypt as his wages, which was not made a forty-year desolation either. Udd and the Bulletin for Biblical Research article treat it as a dated prediction the book itself revises [30]. The judaism report left charging it to Alex because Christianity inherits Ezekiel too; principle 3 resolves that, since Christianity's dated-prediction kind is already charged once and cannot be charged again. Jeremiah's seventy years (Jeremiah 25:11, 29:10) is the candidate held prediction; Winkle's re-assessment shows the figure can be made to fit 605 to 538 or 586 to 516 and that its dating within the book is disputed [31]. Not credited; see Contested calls.
- Open observation: the covenant is stated as permanent; the people's extinction would have ended it visibly, and anyone can observe today that it has not. The survival itself is the continuous community credited under history, so it is not a held kind, as the file says; the kind of refuter still counts.
- Controlled experiment: Deuteronomy 4:7 and the Amidah promise answered petition [32]; STEP and Cochrane are the trials [7][8]. Failed, once.
- Logical demonstration: Mackie's argument that an all-powerful, perfectly good God cannot coexist with any evil is pressed against this God as against every theism (Tooley's survey) [33]. Yes.

**Rule applied:** Exposure 5: historical finding, controlled experiment, dated prediction, open observation, logical demonstration. Held: none outside foundation claims scored under history. Failed: Ezekiel's Tyre prophecy (minus 1); prayer trials (minus 1). Three.

**Proposed:** 2 → 3. Confidence 50. Judaism's central claim is the most exposed in the table because it is historical, present-tense and prophetic at once. The Ezekiel charge is a change to the file's held/failed list; source: Udd (2005) [30], from the judaism report.

why: Exposure 5: a historical finding, the Exodus record, bears on revelation in history; a controlled experiment bears on its promise of answered prayer; its prophets set dates; an observation open to anyone, the people's extinction, would end a permanent covenant; a logical demonstration, the argument from evil, targets its God. Held: none outside foundation claims scored under history. Failed, minus 1 each: Ezekiel's dated Tyre prophecy, which the book itself revises; prayer trials, once against every tradition promising answered prayer. Three.

Sources to add: "Kris J. Udd, Prediction and Foreknowledge in Ezekiel's Prophecy against Tyre, Tyndale Bulletin 56 (2005)"

## Hinduism

**Evidence**
- Historical finding: the central claim names no founder and no event; the epic chronology and Indus-to-Vedic lineage are charged under history but are not the core claim. No.
- Dated prediction: the Puranic yugas fix the Kali Yuga's start at 3102 BCE and its length at 432,000 years, which no check can reach; Kalki is undated. No.
- Controlled experiment and open observation: contemplative claims about attention and the self are trialled (Goyal; Van Dam) with the same weak support Buddhism gets [12][13]. Held, once, as the file has it.
- Logical demonstration: Ramanuja's seven untenabilities against Advaita's identity of self and Brahman [34], and Kaufman's regress and problem-of-evil arguments against karma [35], are standing demonstrations attempted against the core claim. Yes.
- Prayer: the devotional strands promise answered prayer; STEP and Cochrane are the trials [7][8]. Failed, once.
- Rebirth memory: the Leininger re-examination finds the strongest American case rests on parental testimony [36]. Neutral.
- Jyotisha: a double-blind test of 27 astrologers on 200 charts scored below chance [37]; the file treats astrology as a popular practice, not a claim of the worldview. Left in Contested calls.

**Rule applied:** Exposure 3: controlled experiment, open observation, logical demonstration; no historical finding, no dated prediction. Held: contemplative claims (plus 1). Failed: prayer trials (minus 1). Rebirth memory neutral. Three.

**Proposed:** 1 → 3. Confidence 55. The old exposure 1 was set by hand on the ground that Brahman and karma admit no refuter; counting kinds, the contemplative half of the claim admits two and the metaphysical half admits a logical one.

why: Exposure 3: a controlled experiment on contemplative practice, an observation open to anyone about attention and the self, and a logical demonstration that karma or the self's identity with Brahman is incoherent each reach it; it has no founding event for a historical finding, and its dated cycles outrun any check. Held, plus 1: contemplative claims about attention and the self, which practice can test and research partly supports. Failed, minus 1: prayer trials, once against every tradition promising answered prayer, as its devotional strands do. Three.

Sources to add: "Whitley Kaufman, Karma, Rebirth, and the Problem of Evil, Philosophy East and West 55 (2005)"; "Madhav Goyal et al., Meditation Programs for Psychological Stress and Well-being, JAMA Internal Medicine 174 (2014)"

If exposure is limited to the creator half: 1 (logical demonstration only).
- **Deism, failed kind (none).** The fact: Paine's claim that no tree, plant or animal could make itself was a checkable claim about biology that natural selection answered [24]; the deism report proposed minus 1. If charged: 3.
- **Christianity, open observation (no).** The fact: Matthew 24:14 and 16:18 promise the message would reach all nations and the church would not fail, which anyone can observe today; the file refuses the church's survival as a foundation claim under history, and Judaism's survival is refused on the same ground while its extinction still counts as a refuter kind. If the same treatment is given here: exposure 5, score 3.
- **Christianity, held kind (none).** The fact: Mark 13:2 predicts the temple's destruction and Mark's date relative to 70 is unsettled; Islam's Rome prophecy (Q 30:2-4) is credited despite Tesei's identical ex eventu argument relative to 628 [26]. Either credit both or neither. Crediting Mark 13:2: 3. Dropping Rome changes nothing for Islam, since the Hour deduction falls with it under the file's cancel.
- **Islam, open observation (no).** The fact: the Quran itself issues the inimitability challenge as a public test (Q 2:23) [28]. If a test the text issues counts regardless of who can judge it: exposure 5, score 4.
- **Judaism, Ezekiel (charged).** The fact: Ezekiel 29:17-20 revises the Tyre prophecy within the book [30]; the judaism report did not charge it because Christianity inherits Ezekiel, and principle 3 answers that (Christianity's dated-prediction kind is already charged once). If not charged: 4.
- **Judaism, Jeremiah's seventy years (not credited).** The fact: if Ezekiel's failure is charged, Jeremiah 25:11 and 29:10 is a dated prediction of the same kind that held approximately (605 to 538, or 586 to 516), and Winkle argues its referent is Babylon's supremacy [31]; critical dating of the passages is disputed. - **Buddhism, Vinaya five-hundred-year limit (charged, exposure 4).** The fact: Nattier reports scholars generally reluctant to accept the statement as the Buddha's own, and it is absent from Mahasamghika texts [10], whereas critical scholars accept the generation saying as Jesus' own. If not counted: exposure 3, no deduction, still 3.
- **Hinduism, Jyotisha (not charged).** The fact: astrology is one of the six Vedangas and the basis of horoscope matching, and a double-blind test scored it below chance [37]; the rule excludes popular claims. If treated as a claim of the tradition: 2.
